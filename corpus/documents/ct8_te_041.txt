Subject: heads up — Brennan Facilities contract is finalized

Hi all,

Just a quick note before the long weekend since I know a few people have been asking about the loading dock maintenance situation.

First, a little background for anyone who wasn't around for the vendor selection process back in the spring — we looked at four companies total: Brennan Facilities Group, Wyndmere Property Services, Calloway Maintenance Partners, and one smaller local outfit called Reyes & Sons that honestly seemed too small to handle a site our size. Brennan has been doing commercial facilities work in the metro area since 1998 and currently services something like 60 buildings, according to their sales rep, Tomas Ibarra. Their "core values" page talks a lot about "integrity in every corner swept," which made a couple of us laugh during the pitch meeting, but the actual proposal was solid.

Just a reminder before I go further: this email thread and any attached documents contain vendor pricing information that should be treated as internal-only. Please don't share outside the facilities and procurement teams without checking with Legal first — this is the standard confidentiality note we're supposed to include whenever contract numbers get mentioned.

So here's the actual update: we've executed the service agreement with Brennan Facilities Group, effective November 1st. Owen Castellano signed on our behalf as Facilities Director, and their regional manager, Denise Achterberg, signed for Brennan on October 22nd. Under the agreement, Brennan will handle all loading dock maintenance, including quarterly inspections, hydraulic lift servicing, and emergency repair response, for a flat monthly fee of $4,750. The contract runs for an initial 24-month term with automatic renewal unless either party gives 90 days' written notice.

While I have you — totally separate topic — the building management office mentioned that the elevator in the north wing will be down for inspection next Tuesday, so plan accordingly if you're on floors 4 through 7.

Back to Brennan: the agreement specifies that they must respond to emergency service calls within 4 hours during business hours and within 12 hours after hours, and they're required to carry a minimum of $2 million in general liability insurance, which their team already provided proof of. On our side, we're obligated to give at least 24 hours' notice for scheduled maintenance access and to pay invoices within 15 days of receipt, which is a shorter window than we usually negotiate, but it was part of what got us the discounted monthly rate.

Not related to any of this, but I wanted to mention that the cafeteria vendor switched suppliers again, so the salad bar options have changed — apparently there's a new vinaigrette that half the office already has opinions about.

One more relevant detail: the agreement includes a standard indemnification clause and caps Brennan's liability at the total value of fees paid over the preceding twelve months, which our legal team reviewed and approved without changes. There's also a short appendix listing approved subcontractors Brennan may use for specialized hydraulic work, mostly a company called Ferris Lift Technical Services, in case that name comes up on any future invoices.

I'm attaching the signed PDF along with the insurance certificate and the subcontractor appendix for anyone who wants the full record. Let me know if you have questions before Brennan's team does their first walkthrough next Wednesday.
