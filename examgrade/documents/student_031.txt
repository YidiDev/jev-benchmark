Q (q01, 3 pts): Describe the political and administrative structure the Mongol Empire used to govern its territory.
A: The Mongol Empire was founded by Genghis Khan. It governed a huge multi-ethnic population. Mongols didn't rely on old tribal or clan hierarchies. This let them mobilize troops fast. They could command many different peoples at once. It was efficient for their conquests.

Q (q02, 4 pts): Explain the significance of the Indian Ocean trade network before 1500 CE.
A: So the Indian Ocean trade network was huge before 1500, connecting places like East Africa, the Swahili coast, Arabia, India, Southeast Asia, and China, all trading stuff back and forth. Like goods such as spices, textiles, porcelain, gold, all that moved along these routes. And I keep thinking about how the monsoon winds were key to this, because they blew predictably in one direction part of the year and then reversed, so sailors could actually plan round trips instead of just guessing when to sail. That predictability is what really made the whole network work so well, connecting all those regions I mentioned with goods like spices and porcelain moving between them. Yeah the winds and the regions and the goods, that's basically the core of why it mattered so much for connecting all these different areas of the world.

Q (q03, 3 pts): Describe the circumstances of the fall of the Byzantine Empire in 1453.
A: The Ottomans used massive siege cannons to break through Constantinople's ancient walls, which had held for centuries before that. This event marked the end of the Roman imperial line since Byzantium was considered its continuation. It also gave the Ottomans control over key trade routes in the Eastern Mediterranean, which pushed Europeans to look for new routes to Asia instead.

Q (q04, 3 pts): Explain the impact of the Black Death on European society and economy.
A: The Black Death hit in the mid-1300s. It killed about a third of Europe's population. Fewer workers meant labor got scarce. Wages went up. Serfdom weakened. The manorial system started falling apart. Feudalism declined faster because of this. The plague also caused religious panic. People blamed minority groups, like Jews, and attacked them. Prayer didn't stop the disease. This made people question the Church's power and authority.

That's a huge part of why it mattered so much. And thinking about it more, this whole system also really messed up West and Central Africa over time, like it depopulated regions and destabilized societies there for centuries because so many people were taken away. Then on the other side, in the Americas, you get this lasting African influence, like culturally and religiously and even in language, that's still visible today because of all the people who were brought over. So yeah, the triangular trade, the depopulation of Africa, and the cultural blending in the Americas, those are the big things I keep coming back to when I think about why this mattered.

Q (q09, 4 pts): Describe the Columbian Exchange and its effects.

Food supplies grew. Populations grew there too. European livestock and crops changed American ecosystems. Diets changed on both sides. Societies transformed everywhere.

Q (q10, 3 pts): Explain the significance of the Tokugawa Shogunate's sakoku policy.
A: The sakoku policy severely restricted foreign trade and travel. Japan became largely closed off from the outside world during this time. However, it wasn't a total shutdown. The Dutch were still allowed limited trade through Nagasaki, specifically at the controlled port of Dejima. This gave Japan some small window to the outside world while otherwise remaining isolated.

Q (q11, 3 pts): Explain the causes of the Scientific Revolution in Europe.
A: The Scientific Revolution grew out of a new emphasis on empirical observation and experimentation. People wanted to test ideas rather than just accept old authorities. This directly challenged reliance on classical texts like Aristotle's works. It also pushed back against religious doctrine being treated as the only source of truth about the natural world.

Q (q12, 4 pts): Explain how Enlightenment political philosophy influenced later revolutions.

These ideas directly fed into the French Revolution. Revolutionaries there called for liberty, equality, and an end to absolute monarchy, drawing on these philosophers' arguments.

Q (q18, 3 pts): Explain the significance of the Meiji Restoration in Japan.
