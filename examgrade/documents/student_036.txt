Q (q08, 3 pts): Explain the causes of the Protestant Reformation.
A: The Protestant Reformation began largely because of Martin Luther, who in 1517 wrote his Ninety-Five Theses criticizing practices within the Catholic Church. A central issue was his objection to the sale of indulgences, along with broader anger at what many saw as corruption among Church officials. His ideas were able to spread quickly because of the printing press, which allowed pamphlets and translations to reach a wide audience across Europe. In addition, several German princes chose to support the reform movement for their own political and economic reasons, such as gaining independence from the authority of the Catholic Church and Holy Roman Emperor.

Q (q10, 3 pts): Explain the significance of the Tokugawa Shogunate's sakoku policy.
A: The Tokugawa Shogunate's sakoku policy was meant to prevent the spread of Christianity in Japan and to limit foreign political and economic influence on Japanese society. However, the policy was not a total lockdown, since limited Dutch trade was still allowed through the controlled port of Nagasaki, specifically on the island of Dejima. This let Japan keep some contact with European knowledge and goods while still trying to control outside influence closely.

Q (q12, 4 pts): Explain how Enlightenment political philosophy influenced later revolutions.
A: Enlightenment philosophy introduced key political concepts like natural rights, the social contract, and separation of powers. These ideas gave people a new framework for thinking about government and the relationship between rulers and the ruled. They also directly shaped the French Revolution, since revolutionaries called for liberty, equality, and an end to absolute monarchy, drawing heavily on these Enlightenment concepts.

Q (q13, 5 pts): Describe the causes and effects of the French Revolution.
A: The French Revolution had several causes. One major cause was resentment of the rigid estates system, since the nobility and clergy enjoyed privileges while the common people bore most of the burdens. Another cause was the spread of Enlightenment ideas about liberty, equality, and popular sovereignty, which made people question the fairness of absolute monarchy. In terms of effects, the instability that followed the revolution eventually created the conditions for Napoleon Bonaparte to rise to power.
