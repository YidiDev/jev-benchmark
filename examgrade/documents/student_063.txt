Q (q01, 3 pts): Describe the political and administrative structure the Mongol Empire used to govern its territory.
A: Mongols organized people into units of 10. Also 100. Also 1,000. Also 10,000. This cut across old tribal and clan lines. Broke old loyalties apart. Let them command huge groups of different peoples fast. No need for old tribal chiefs. Just move units around.

Q (q02, 4 pts): Explain the significance of the Indian Ocean trade network before 1500 CE.
A: So the Indian Ocean trade network was a big deal because it connected places like East Africa, especially the Swahili coast, with Arabia, India, Southeast Asia, and China, all trading stuff like spices and textiles and porcelain and gold back and forth. Like I said it linked all these regions together, East Africa to Arabia to India to China, moving spices, cloth, porcelain, gold, all that. And because of this trade contact, Islam actually spread along the East African coast and into Southeast Asia, since merchants and travelers carried their religion with them as they traded, so it wasn't just goods moving but also religion and ideas. Again just to restate, the network tied together East Africa, Arabia, India, Southeast Asia, China through trade in spices, textiles, porcelain and gold, and Islam spread through merchant contact along the coasts.

Q (q03, 3 pts): Describe the circumstances of the fall of the Byzantine Empire in 1453.
A: Ottomans led by Mehmed II took Constantinople in 1453. They used giant cannons. Broke the old walls that had stood for centuries. City fell after siege. Ended the Roman imperial line for good. Ottomans then controlled key trade routes in the eastern Mediterranean. This pushed Europeans to look for new routes to Asia instead.

Q (q07, 5 pts): Explain the significance of the Atlantic Slave Trade for the development of the Americas.
A: The Atlantic Slave Trade connected Europe, West Africa, and the Americas in a triangular pattern that forcibly moved millions of enslaved Africans across the ocean. These enslaved people were put to work on plantations growing cash crops like sugar, tobacco, and cotton. The profits from these plantation exports created massive wealth for European trading empires and their colonial economies. At the same time, the constant removal of people over centuries depopulated and destabilized large parts of West and Central Africa.

Q (q13, 5 pts): Describe the causes and effects of the French Revolution.
A: So the French Revolution had a bunch of causes going on at once, like the government was basically broke from all this debt, partly from fighting expensive wars, so there was this huge fiscal crisis. And then on top of that people were really resentful of the estates system, like the nobility and clergy got all these privileges and didn't pay taxes while everyone else struggled, so that built up a lot of anger. And then Enlightenment ideas were floating around too, stuff about liberty and equality and popular sovereignty, like the idea that power should come from the people not just a king, and that gave people the language and ideas to actually push back. So basically it's debt and war costs, plus resentment of the estates system, plus Enlightenment ideas, all combining together to set things off.

Q (q15, 4 pts): Explain the process of industrialization in Britain.
A: British industrialization was powered largely by the steam engine, which ran new machinery and later drove railways and steamships. Production shifted away from home-based cottage industry toward large centralized factories where work could be done faster and on a bigger scale. Britain also had abundant domestic coal and iron deposits, which supplied the fuel and materials needed to fuel this growth. As factories multiplied, workers rapidly urbanized, moving into industrial cities that often had harsh and crowded living conditions.

Q (q16, 4 pts): Explain the causes of European imperialism in Africa in the late 19th century.
A: European imperialism in Africa was driven partly by competition and prestige rivalry between the European powers, which they tried to manage through events like the Berlin Conference so they wouldn't go to war over territory. It was also made possible by military technology, especially things like the Maxim gun, which gave European armies a huge advantage over African forces they encountered.

Q (q17, 3 pts): Describe the effects of the Opium Wars on China.
A: Britain sold opium to China. China tried to stop it. Fighting broke out. China lost. Had to sign unequal treaties. Treaty of Nanjing was one. Gave up Hong Kong. Opened treaty ports. Foreigners controlled them.

Q (q18, 3 pts): Explain the significance of the Meiji Restoration in Japan.
A: So the Meiji Restoration basically ended the Tokugawa Shogunate and brought back the emperor as the formal ruler, like restoring imperial rule after all those years of shogun control. And then once that happened Japan went through this huge push to modernize really fast, building up its military, its industries, and its government all based on Western models, trying to catch up with European powers. So it's really about ending the shogunate and restoring the emperor, and then this rapid modernization across military, industry and government following Western styles, that's the core of it I think.

Q (q19, 4 pts): Explain the causes of World War I.
A: World War I broke out due to a tangled system of alliances, which meant a conflict between just two powers could quickly pull in the rest of Europe. Long-standing nationalist ambitions and imperial rivalries had also built up ongoing tension among the great powers for years beforehand. The immediate spark was the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand in Sarajevo in 1914, which set the alliance system into motion.

Q (q20, 4 pts): Explain the significance of the Bolshevik Revolution.
A: Lenin led the Bolsheviks. Took power in 1917. Overthrew the provisional government. That government had replaced the Tsar earlier that year. After taking power, Russia pulled out of World War I. Started building a Communist state.

Q (q21, 4 pts): Describe the causes and consequences of the Great Depression.
A: Great Depression started with the 1929 U.S. stock market crash. Global trade and lending were tightly connected. Crisis spread fast to other countries. Mass unemployment followed. Severe hardship worldwide. This desperation helped fascist and authoritarian movements rise in several countries.

Q (q22, 4 pts): Explain the causes of World War II in Europe.
A: Resentment over the Treaty of Versailles fueled German nationalism after World War I, as many Germans felt humiliated by its harsh terms. This resentment fed into the rise of Hitler and Nazi ideology, which pursued aggressive territorial expansion across Europe. Western powers responded with a policy of appeasement, but this failed to stop Germany's escalating aggression.
