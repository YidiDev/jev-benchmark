Q (q01, 3 pts): Describe the political and administrative structure the Mongol Empire used to govern its territory.
A: The empire ran on a decimal system. People got split into groups of 10, 100, 1,000, and 10,000. This cut across old tribal and clan lines. It let rulers move and command huge numbers of different peoples fast. No need to depend on old tribal chiefs or loyalties. Just numbers and command structure.

Q (q07, 5 pts): Explain the significance of the Atlantic Slave Trade for the development of the Americas.
A: The Atlantic Slave Trade connected Europe, West Africa, and the Americas in what's called the triangular trade. Millions of enslaved Africans were forcibly transported across the Atlantic as part of this system. Once in the Americas, they were forced to work on plantations growing cash crops like sugar, tobacco, and cotton. The wealth generated from these plantation exports was enormous and helped build up European trading empires and colonial economies. At the same time, this trade depopulated and destabilized parts of West and Central Africa over the centuries it operated.

A: Okay so sakoku, I think this was the policy where Japan basically shut itself off from most of the outside world under the Tokugawa Shogunate. Trade and travel with foreigners was really restricted, like severely limited, so Japan became pretty isolated for a long time. I think part of the reason for doing this was that the Shogunate wanted to stop Christianity from spreading in Japan, and also they wanted to limit how much foreign powers could influence Japan politically and economically. So it kind of circles back to this idea of control, keeping outside influence out to protect their own power structure.

Q (q15, 4 pts): Explain the process of industrialization in Britain.
A: Britain's industrialization relied heavily on the steam engine. This technology powered new machinery in factories and eventually powered railways and ships too. Along with this came a shift away from the old cottage industry model, where people made goods at home, toward centralized factories where production was concentrated in one place. This combination of new power sources and new organization of labor drove the industrial process forward.
