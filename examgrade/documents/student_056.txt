Q (q01, 3 pts): Describe the political and administrative structure the Mongol Empire used to govern its territory.
A: The empire organized its people using a decimal-based system, breaking the population into units of 10, 100, 1,000, and 10,000. This structure cut across old tribal and clan loyalties, so people were no longer grouped by their birth clans. Because of this, the rulers could rapidly mobilize huge numbers of soldiers and administer a massive, multi-ethnic population without having to depend on pre-existing tribal hierarchies that might compete for power.

Also religion spread along these routes, especially Islam, which merchants and travelers carried down the East African coast and into Southeast Asia as they traded, so it wasn't just goods moving but ideas and beliefs too. So yeah, goods, wind patterns, and religious spread, that's basically what made this network so significant, even though I keep feeling like there's more to say about it.

Q (q03, 3 pts): Describe the circumstances of the fall of the Byzantine Empire in 1453.
A: In 1453 the Ottoman Empire, led by Sultan Mehmed II, successfully captured the city of Constantinople after a long siege. The Ottomans were able to breach the city's ancient and previously impenetrable walls by using massive siege cannons, which were a major military innovation for the time. This conquest marked the definitive end of the Roman imperial line, since Byzantium had considered itself the continuation of Rome, and it gave the Ottomans control over crucial trade routes linking Europe and Asia in the Eastern Mediterranean. This loss of easy access to Asian trade pushed European powers to search for alternative sea routes to Asia, which would eventually lead to the Age of Exploration.

Q (q04, 3 pts): Explain the impact of the Black Death on European society and economy.
A: The Black Death killed about a third of the population of Europe in the mid-1300s. Because so many workers died, there was a severe labor shortage afterward, and surviving peasants and laborers were able to demand higher wages. This weakened the manorial system and serfdom, since lords could no longer bind workers to the land as easily, helping speed up the decline of feudalism in Europe.

A: Tribute system. Conquered cities had to pay goods. Also labor. Also soldiers for war. This tribute supported Tenochtitlan. It supported the elite too. Aztecs didn't rule directly. No need for full administration everywhere. Just tribute demands.

Q (q06, 3 pts): Explain how the Ottoman Empire used the devshirme system.
A: So the devshirme system was basically this practice the Ottomans used where they would periodically take Christian boys from the Balkan provinces, like a levy or a tax but in people instead of goods. These boys were then converted to Islam and put through training, and a lot of them ended up becoming Janissary soldiers, who were this elite fighting force, or sometimes they rose up to become high administrators in the government. What this really did, and I keep coming back to this point, is it gave the Sultan a military and bureaucratic class that owed loyalty directly to him personally rather than to some hereditary noble family that might have its own agenda. So instead of relying on nobles who could challenge his authority, the Sultan built up this whole class of loyal servants through devshirme, which strengthened his personal control over the empire.

Q (q09, 4 pts): Describe the Columbian Exchange and its effects.
A: The Columbian Exchange refers to the transfer of crops, livestock, people, and diseases between the Americas and Afro-Eurasia after 1492. One major effect was that Old World diseases like smallpox spread to indigenous populations in the Americas, who had no prior immunity, causing devastating population losses. At the same time, European livestock and crops were introduced to the Americas and transformed local ecosystems and diets, while American crops also reshaped societies overseas.

Q (q10, 3 pts): Explain the significance of the Tokugawa Shogunate's sakoku policy.
A: The sakoku policy of the Tokugawa Shogunate severely limited foreign trade and travel, effectively closing Japan off from most contact with the outside world. However, it was not a complete isolation, since limited trade with the Dutch was still allowed, restricted to the controlled port of Nagasaki at the artificial island of Dejima.

Q (q11, 3 pts): Explain the causes of the Scientific Revolution in Europe.
A: The Scientific Revolution challenged the long-standing reliance on classical texts, like the works of Aristotle, as well as religious doctrine, as the only accepted sources of truth about the natural world. Figures such as Copernicus, Galileo, and Newton made major discoveries, like the heliocentric model of the solar system, that contradicted older teachings and pushed people to question established authorities.

Q (q12, 4 pts): Explain how Enlightenment political philosophy influenced later revolutions.
A: Enlightenment thinkers like Locke, Rousseau, and Montesquieu developed political ideas that had a huge influence on later revolutions. Their arguments about self-government and natural rights directly shaped the reasoning behind the American Revolution, as colonists used these ideas to justify breaking away from British rule. Similarly, these same kinds of ideas fed directly into the French Revolution, inspiring calls for liberty, equality, and an end to absolute monarchy.

Q (q13, 5 pts): Describe the causes and effects of the French Revolution.
A: One major cause of the French Revolution was the chronic debt of the French government, made much worse by years of costly wars, which left the state in a fiscal crisis. Another cause was widespread resentment of the rigid estates system, since the nobility and clergy held onto special privileges while the common people bore most of the tax burden. These pressures eventually led to the monarchy being overthrown, and King Louis XVI was executed. The instability and power vacuum that followed in the years after ultimately allowed Napoleon Bonaparte to rise to power.

Q (q14, 4 pts): Explain the significance of the Haitian Revolution.
A: The Haitian Revolution was significant for several reasons. It was led by Toussaint Louverture, who helped organize the enslaved population into a successful uprising against colonial control. As a result of the revolution, France lost Saint-Domingue, its most profitable colony, which weakened France's overall position in the Americas. The revolution also inspired enslaved and colonized people elsewhere to imagine their own paths to freedom, while at the same time it alarmed other slaveholding powers who feared similar uprisings.

Q (q27, 4 pts): Describe the causes and effects of the fall of the Soviet Union.
A: One major cause of the fall of the Soviet Union was long-term economic stagnation, since the centrally planned economy had become deeply inefficient and could not keep up with Western economies.
