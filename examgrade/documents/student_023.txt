A: The fall of the Byzantine Empire in 1453 was a really significant turning point because it brought an end to the Roman imperial line that had technically continued through Byzantium for centuries. It also meant that control over important trade routes in the Eastern Mediterranean shifted to new hands, which made it harder and more expensive for Europeans to access Asian goods the way they had before. This pushed European states to start looking for alternative routes to Asia, which eventually contributed to the Age of Exploration.
