Q (q03, 3 pts): Describe the circumstances of the fall of the Byzantine Empire in 1453.
A: The Byzantine Empire fell in 1453 when the Ottoman Empire, led by Mehmed II, captured its capital city of Constantinople. The Ottomans achieved this using massive siege cannons that were able to breach the city's ancient and previously impenetrable walls, which had protected the city for centuries. This conquest marked the definitive end of the Roman imperial line, since Byzantium had considered itself the continuation of Rome. It also gave the Ottomans control over key Eastern Mediterranean trade routes, which cut off or complicated European access to Asian goods and helped spur Europeans to search for new sea routes to Asia, contributing to the Age of Exploration.

Q (q04, 3 pts): Explain the impact of the Black Death on European society and economy.
A: The Black Death killed roughly a third of Europe's population in the mid-1300s. This massive population loss caused a labor shortage afterward. Because workers were now scarce, wages rose and peasants had more leverage, which weakened the manorial system and serfdom. This helped accelerate the decline of feudalism in Europe.

Q (q12, 4 pts): Explain how Enlightenment political philosophy influenced later revolutions.
A: Enlightenment thinkers introduced ideas like natural rights, the social contract, and separation of powers. These concepts came from philosophers such as Locke, Rousseau, and Montesquieu. Their writings directly shaped the French Revolution, since revolutionaries used these ideas to call for liberty, equality, and an end to absolute monarchy. Overall these political philosophies gave later revolutionary movements a language and justification for challenging traditional authority.

Q (q14, 4 pts): Explain the significance of the Haitian Revolution.
A: The Haitian Revolution was significant because Toussaint Louverture led the enslaved population in rising up against their oppressors. This uprising inspired enslaved and colonized people elsewhere to imagine their own paths to freedom. At the same time, it alarmed slaveholding powers around the Atlantic world, since it showed that an enslaved population could successfully rebel. These ripple effects made it an important moment for both hope and fear across the Americas.

Q (q19, 4 pts): Explain the causes of World War I.
A: One major cause of World War I was the alliance system in Europe. Because of these alliances, a conflict between just two powers could quickly pull in the rest of the continent. Another cause was the arms race happening before the war, especially the naval competition between Britain and Germany. This buildup of weapons and readiness for war made countries more willing and prepared to fight once tensions rose.

Q (q20, 4 pts): Explain the significance of the Bolshevik Revolution.
A: Vladimir Lenin and the Bolshevik Party seized power in Russia in 1917. Once in power, they pulled Russia out of World War I and began building a Communist state. This made Russia the site of the world's first major Communist state, which inspired revolutionary movements elsewhere while also alarming capitalist governments around the world. It was a turning point that shaped much of twentieth century politics.

Q (q21, 4 pts): Describe the causes and consequences of the Great Depression.
A: The Great Depression was triggered by the 1929 U.S. stock market crash. This led to mass unemployment and severe economic hardship not just in America but worldwide. The desperation caused by these hard times contributed to the rise of authoritarian and fascist movements in several countries, as people turned to extreme leaders promising solutions. This combination of economic collapse and political radicalization had lasting global consequences.
