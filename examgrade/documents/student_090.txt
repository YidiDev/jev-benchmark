Q (q01, 3 pts): Describe the political and administrative structure the Mongol Empire used to govern its territory.
A: The Mongol Empire was founded by Genghis Khan, who unified the steppe tribes under his rule. To govern and organize his forces, he set up a decimal-based military-administrative system that grouped people into units of 10, 100, 1,000, and 10,000, which cut across the old tribal and clan loyalties that had previously divided the steppe peoples. Because loyalty was now owed to the unit and its commander rather than to one's birth clan, the Mongols could quickly mobilize and command a huge, multi-ethnic population without depending on pre-existing tribal hierarchies, giving them an efficient and flexible system for administering their vast conquests.

Q (q02, 4 pts): Explain the significance of the Indian Ocean trade network before 1500 CE.
A: Indian Ocean trade connected East Africa, Arabia, India, Southeast Asia, and China. Sailors used the monsoon winds. These winds reversed seasonally, so ships could plan round trips reliably. This network also spread Islam. Merchants and travelers brought Islam to the Swahili coast and to Southeast Asia.

Q (q04, 3 pts): Explain the impact of the Black Death on European society and economy.
A: I think the Black Death had a really massive impact on Europe, killing something like a third of the entire population in the mid-1300s, though I'm not totally sure of the exact number. Because so many workers died, I believe there was a labor shortage afterward, which drove wages up since laborers were now in demand, and this ended up weakening the manorial system and serfdom, kind of speeding along the decline of feudalism. I also think it caused a lot of religious turmoil, like people turned to scapegoating minority groups such as Jewish communities, and there was growing doubt about the Church's authority since prayer and religious devotion didn't seem to stop the plague from spreading.

Q (q05, 2 pts): Describe how the Aztec Empire extracted resources from the peoples it conquered.
A: The Aztec Empire relied heavily on a tribute system. Conquered city-states were required to regularly send goods, labor, or soldiers to the Aztec rulers. This meant that conquered peoples had ongoing obligations to the empire even after being defeated militarily.

Q (q09, 4 pts): Describe the Columbian Exchange and its effects.
A: The Columbian Exchange refers to the transfer of crops, livestock, people, and diseases between the Americas and Afro-Eurasia after 1492. This exchange went both ways across the Atlantic. New World crops like maize and potatoes were especially important because they boosted food supplies in Afro-Eurasia, which helped populations grow there over time.

Q (q10, 3 pts): Explain the significance of the Tokugawa Shogunate's sakoku policy.
A: Sakoku severely restricted foreign trade and travel for Japan, largely cutting the country off from outside contact. However, it wasn't a total isolation, since limited trade with the Dutch was still allowed through the controlled port of Nagasaki, specifically at Dejima island.

A: New emphasis on observation. Experiments mattered more. People wanted evidence. Copernicus and Galileo pushed heliocentrism. Newton too. These discoveries changed how people saw the universe.

Q (q12, 4 pts): Explain how Enlightenment political philosophy influenced later revolutions.
A: Enlightenment thinkers pushed ideas like natural rights, the social contract, and separation of powers. Locke, Rousseau, and Montesquieu were key figures behind these concepts. These ideas fed directly into the American Revolution, giving colonists arguments for self-government and independence from Britain.

Q (q13, 5 pts): Describe the causes and effects of the French Revolution.
A: The French Revolution had several causes. First, the government was deeply in debt, made worse by expensive wars, creating a fiscal crisis. Second, there was resentment of the estates system, since nobility and clergy had privileges that commoners lacked. Third, Enlightenment ideas about liberty, equality, and popular sovereignty spread and gave people a language to demand change.

Q (q14, 4 pts): Explain the significance of the Haitian Revolution.
A: The Haitian Revolution was significant because it was the only successful large-scale slave revolt that led to an independent nation, Haiti. Because of this, France lost its most profitable colony, which weakened its overall position in the Americas. The revolution also inspired enslaved and colonized peoples elsewhere while alarming slaveholding powers who feared similar uprisings.

Q (q15, 4 pts): Explain the process of industrialization in Britain.
A: Industrialization in Britain led to rapid urbanization. Workers moved into growing industrial cities to find jobs. Conditions in these cities were often harsh and overcrowded for the people living there.

Q (q17, 3 pts): Describe the effects of the Opium Wars on China.
A: China was militarily defeated by Britain, and later other powers got involved as well. As a result, China was forced into unequal treaties like the Treaty of Nanjing, which meant ceding Hong Kong and opening up treaty ports where foreign powers had control.

Q (q18, 3 pts): Explain the significance of the Meiji Restoration in Japan.
A: The Meiji Restoration ended the Tokugawa Shogunate and formally restored imperial rule in Japan. Following this, Japan rapidly modernized its military, industry, and government institutions based on Western models. As a result, Japan became a major industrial and military power, managing to avoid colonization by Western powers and later projecting its own imperial power over other nations.

Q (q19, 4 pts): Explain the causes of World War I.
A: One cause of WWI was the alliance system. Because of this web of alliances, a conflict between two powers could quickly pull in the rest of Europe. Another cause was the arms race before the war, especially the naval competition between Britain and Germany, which made all the powers more ready and willing to go to war.

Q (q20, 4 pts): Explain the significance of the Bolshevik Revolution.
A: The Bolshevik Revolution was significant because Vladimir Lenin and the Bolshevik Party seized power in 1917. They overthrew the provisional government, which had itself replaced the Tsar earlier that same year. After taking power, Russia withdrew from World War I and began building a Communist state. This created the world's first major Communist state, which went on to inspire revolutionary movements elsewhere while also alarming capitalist and conservative governments worldwide.
