Q (q01, 3 pts): Describe the political and administrative structure the Mongol Empire used to govern its territory.
A: I think the Mongols organized their people using some kind of decimal system, like grouping soldiers and families into units of 10, 100, 1,000, and 10,000. I'm not totally sure but I believe this was meant to break up old tribal and clan loyalties so that people were loyal to their unit or leader instead of their birth clan. It seems like this system was a big part of how they managed to control so much territory with so many different peoples under them.

Q (q02, 4 pts): Explain the significance of the Indian Ocean trade network before 1500 CE.
A: The Indian Ocean trade network carried a wide variety of goods, including spices, textiles, porcelain, and gold, moving between many ports. This trade also helped spread Islam, as merchants and travelers brought their faith to places like the East African coast and parts of Southeast Asia. Local rulers and communities in these areas gradually adopted Islam through contact with these traders. This religious spread was one of the lasting cultural effects of the network.

Q (q07, 5 pts): Explain the significance of the Atlantic Slave Trade for the development of the Americas.
A: I think the Atlantic Slave Trade was significant in several connected ways. There was the triangular trade that linked Europe, West Africa, and the Americas, and through this system millions of enslaved Africans were forcibly transported across the Atlantic. Once in the Americas, I believe most of them were forced into labor on plantations growing cash crops like sugar, tobacco, and cotton. The wealth generated by exporting these plantation goods was enormous, and I think it really helped build up the economic power of European trading empires and their colonies. At the same time, this trade caused a lot of depopulation and instability in parts of West and Central Africa over the centuries it went on, since so many people were taken away. Finally, I'm fairly confident that it also left a lasting mark on the Americas culturally, since African traditions, religions, and languages blended into and influenced American societies in ways that are still visible today.

Q (q08, 3 pts): Explain the causes of the Protestant Reformation.
A: So thinking about the Protestant Reformation, I keep coming back to Martin Luther and his Ninety-Five Theses in 1517, which was basically him writing up all his complaints against the Catholic Church, and that document is usually seen as the starting point. A big part of what he was upset about, and I guess this is worth restating, was the selling of indulgences, which he and a lot of other people saw as corrupt, along with other problems within the Church at the time. Then there's the printing press, which I think is really important here too because it let Luther's ideas spread across Europe way faster than they would have otherwise, reaching way more people than just word of mouth ever could. And on top of that, some German princes decided to back the reform movement, but not just for religious reasons, more because it worked out well for them politically and economically to break away from the authority of the Church and the Holy Roman Emperor. So altogether, between Luther's theses, the anger over indulgences and corruption, and the printing press spreading it all while princes had their own incentives to support it, that's basically what caused the Reformation.

Q (q09, 4 pts): Describe the Columbian Exchange and its effects.
A: The Columbian Exchange refers to the transfer of crops, livestock, people, and diseases between the Americas and Afro-Eurasia after 1492. One major effect was that Old World diseases such as smallpox spread among indigenous populations in the Americas, who had no immunity and suffered massive population loss as a result. Another major effect was that European livestock and crops were introduced to the Americas, which transformed local ecosystems and diets there. At the same time, American crops moving to the Old World reshaped agriculture and food habits in Europe, Africa, and Asia as well.

Q (q17, 3 pts): Describe the effects of the Opium Wars on China.
A: China was militarily defeated in the Opium Wars by Britain and later by other foreign powers as well. As a result, China was forced into signing unequal treaties, such as the Treaty of Nanjing, which required ceding territory like Hong Kong and opening up treaty ports that came under foreign control. These treaties significantly undermined Chinese sovereignty over its own trade and territory.

Q (q18, 3 pts): Explain the significance of the Meiji Restoration in Japan.
A: The Meiji Restoration ended the Tokugawa Shogunate and formally restored imperial rule in Japan. Following this change, Japan rapidly modernized its military, industry, and government institutions, largely modeling them on Western examples. This transformation happened over a relatively short period of time.
