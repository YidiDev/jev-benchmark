Q (q01, 3 pts): Describe the political and administrative structure the Mongol Empire used to govern its territory.
A: The Mongol Empire was founded by Genghis Khan, who unified the steppe tribes under his rule. To govern and mobilize such a vast territory, he organized the population into a decimal-based military-administrative system, dividing people into units of 10, 100, 1,000, and 10,000. This system deliberately cut across old tribal and clan loyalties, breaking down traditional divisions. As a result, the Mongols could rapidly mobilize and command a huge multi-ethnic population without needing to rely on pre-existing tribal hierarchies.

Q (q02, 4 pts): Explain the significance of the Indian Ocean trade network before 1500 CE.
A: The Indian Ocean trade network before 1500 CE connected East Africa's Swahili coast, Arabia, India, Southeast Asia, and China into a vast commercial system. Merchants exchanged valuable goods such as spices, textiles, porcelain, and gold across these regions. This trade was made possible by the predictable seasonal reversal of the monsoon winds, which allowed sailors to plan reliable round-trip voyages. Beyond economic exchange, the network also facilitated the spread of Islam along the East African coast and into Southeast Asia as Muslim merchants and travelers settled and interacted with local populations.

Q (q04, 3 pts): Explain the impact of the Black Death on European society and economy.
A: So the Black Death, which hit Europe in the mid-1300s, ended up killing like a third of the population, which is just a massive number when you think about it, like a third of everyone just gone. Because of that huge population loss there was suddenly a labor shortage, so peasants and workers could demand higher wages since there weren't enough people to work the land, and this ended up weakening the whole manorial and serfdom system, kind of speeding up the decline of feudalism that was already happening slowly. And then also, going back to the disease itself, it caused a lot of religious panic because people prayed for it to stop and it didn't, so people started blaming minority groups, like scapegoating Jewish communities, and also started questioning the Church's authority since it couldn't explain or stop the plague. So overall it was demographic, economic, and religious all at once.

Q (q08, 3 pts): Explain the causes of the Protestant Reformation.
A: The Protestant Reformation was sparked in 1517 when Martin Luther wrote his Ninety-Five Theses criticizing the Catholic Church. His main objections were to the sale of indulgences and what he saw as widespread corruption within the Church. These ideas spread rapidly because of the printing press, which allowed pamphlets and translations to reach a wide audience quickly. Additionally, some German princes chose to support the reform movement for their own political and economic reasons, such as gaining independence from imperial and papal authority.

Q (q09, 4 pts): Describe the Columbian Exchange and its effects.
A: The Columbian Exchange refers to the transfer of crops, livestock, people, and diseases between the Americas and Afro-Eurasia after Columbus's voyages in 1492. Old World diseases like smallpox devastated indigenous American populations, who had no prior immunity and suffered catastrophic losses. On the other hand, New World crops such as maize and potatoes boosted food supplies in Afro-Eurasia, contributing to population growth there. Meanwhile, European livestock and crops introduced to the Americas transformed local ecosystems and diets, reshaping societies and environments on both sides of the Atlantic.

Q (q12, 4 pts): Explain how Enlightenment political philosophy influenced later revolutions.
A: Okay so Enlightenment political philosophy, thinkers like Locke, Rousseau, and Montesquieu, came up with ideas about natural rights, the social contract, and separation of powers, and these ideas ended up being huge for later revolutions. Like, the natural rights idea, that people have rights just by being human, that fed into stuff like the social contract, which is basically the idea that government only has authority because the people agree to it. And separation of powers, that's more Montesquieu, the idea of splitting government into branches so no one part gets too powerful. Anyway these ideas directly shaped the American Revolution, because the colonists used arguments about natural rights and self-government to justify breaking away from Britain, like in the Declaration of Independence basically restates Locke. And then also, coming back to this, the French Revolution used these same kinds of ideas, liberty, equality, ending absolute monarchy, to justify overthrowing the king and the old order. So really the same set of Enlightenment ideas fed into both revolutions even though they played out really differently.
