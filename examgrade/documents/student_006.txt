Q (q01, 3 pts): Describe the political and administrative structure the Mongol Empire used to govern its territory.
A: The Mongol Empire, founded by Genghis Khan, governed its vast territory through a distinctive decimal-based military-administrative system. People were organized into units of 10, 100, 1,000, and 10,000, which deliberately cut across old tribal and clan loyalties. This structure allowed the Mongols to rapidly mobilize and command a huge, multi-ethnic population without depending on pre-existing tribal hierarchies, giving the empire flexibility and unity despite its massive size.

Q (q04, 3 pts): Explain the impact of the Black Death on European society and economy.
A: The Black Death caused a severe labor shortage across Europe. This drove up wages for surviving workers and weakened the manorial system, which sped up the decline of feudalism since serfs had more bargaining power. It also caused a religious crisis, since prayer did not stop the disease, leading many to question the Church's authority. This crisis also led to scapegoating of minority groups, such as Jewish communities, who were blamed for the plague.

Q (q06, 3 pts): Explain how the Ottoman Empire used the devshirme system.
A: The devshirme system was a periodic levy where the Ottomans took Christian boys from the Balkan provinces of the empire. These boys were then converted to Islam and given extensive training. Many of them went on to become elite Janissary soldiers, while others rose to become high-ranking administrators in the Ottoman government.

Q (q07, 5 pts): Explain the significance of the Atlantic Slave Trade for the development of the Americas.
A: The Atlantic Slave Trade shaped the Americas by supplying forced labor for plantations that produced cash crops like sugar, tobacco, and cotton. These plantation exports generated enormous wealth for European trading empires and their colonial economies. On the African side, the slave trade depopulated and destabilized parts of West and Central Africa over the course of several centuries. It also left a lasting legacy in the Americas through African cultural, religious, and linguistic influence that persists today.

Q (q11, 3 pts): Explain the causes of the Scientific Revolution in Europe.
A: The Scientific Revolution was driven by a new emphasis on empirical observation and experimentation as the proper basis for knowledge, rather than relying purely on tradition. Figures such as Copernicus, Galileo, and Newton made key discoveries, including the heliocentric model of the solar system, which reshaped how people understood the natural world. These new methods and discoveries marked a major shift in how Europeans approached science.

Q (q18, 3 pts): Explain the significance of the Meiji Restoration in Japan.
A: The Meiji Restoration ended the Tokugawa Shogunate and formally restored imperial rule in Japan. Under the new government, Japan rapidly modernized its military, industry, and political institutions, largely modeling them on Western nations. As a result, Japan became a major industrial and military power, which allowed it to avoid colonization by Western powers and eventually to project its own imperial power over other parts of Asia.

Q (q27, 4 pts): Describe the causes and effects of the fall of the Soviet Union.
A: The fall of the Soviet Union resulted from long-term economic stagnation and inefficiency within its centrally planned economy. Mikhail Gorbachev's reform policies of glasnost, meaning openness, and perestroika, meaning restructuring, further destabilized the system by allowing more criticism and change than the government could control. At the same time, rising nationalist movements within various Soviet republics pushed for independence from Moscow. Ultimately, the collapse ended the Cold War and dismantled the bipolar global power structure that had defined international relations for decades.
