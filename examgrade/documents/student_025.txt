Q (q03, 3 pts): Describe the circumstances of the fall of the Byzantine Empire in 1453.
A: In 1453, the Ottoman Empire under Sultan Mehmed II captured the city of Constantinople, bringing an end to the Byzantine Empire. The Ottomans achieved this using massive siege cannons that were able to breach the city's ancient, previously impenetrable walls. This event marked the end of the Roman imperial line that had continued through Byzantium for centuries. It also gave the Ottomans control over key trade routes in the Eastern Mediterranean, which pushed European powers to search for alternative sea routes to Asia.

Q (q05, 2 pts): Describe how the Aztec Empire extracted resources from the peoples it conquered.
A: The Aztec Empire used a tribute system to extract resources from conquered peoples. Under this system, conquered city-states were required to regularly send goods, labor, or soldiers to the empire. This created a steady flow of resources from subject territories to the Aztec core.

Q (q07, 5 pts): Explain the significance of the Atlantic Slave Trade for the development of the Americas.
A: The Atlantic Slave Trade was significant because it created a triangular trade system linking Europe, West Africa, and the Americas, through which millions of enslaved Africans were forcibly transported across the Atlantic. Once in the Americas, these enslaved people were forced to labor on plantations that produced cash crops like sugar, tobacco, and cotton. The wealth generated by these plantation exports was enormous, and it enriched European trading empires as well as the colonial economies that depended on this forced labor.

Q (q08, 3 pts): Explain the causes of the Protestant Reformation.
A: One major cause of the Protestant Reformation was Martin Luther posting his Ninety-Five Theses in 1517, which criticized many practices of the Catholic Church. A key part of his objection was the sale of indulgences, along with broader concerns about corruption within the Church. These criticisms sparked widespread debate and eventually a break from Catholic authority.

Q (q09, 4 pts): Describe the Columbian Exchange and its effects.
A: Okay so the Columbian Exchange, I keep thinking about this in circles but let me just lay it out. Basically when the Old World and New World connected up, diseases like smallpox from the Old World just devastated the indigenous populations in the Americas because they had never been exposed before and had no immunity, so it wiped out huge numbers of people. And then going the other way, crops from the New World like maize and potatoes went over to Afro-Eurasia and actually boosted food supplies there, which helped populations grow over there too. And then also, European livestock and crops that got brought over to the Americas ended up transforming the ecosystems there, like changing the land and changing what people ate, so it reshaped societies on both sides of the ocean, not just one side. So yeah, disease going one way, crops boosting population the other way, and livestock/crops changing the Americas itself, all part of the exchange reshaping both worlds.

Q (q11, 3 pts): Explain the causes of the Scientific Revolution in Europe.
A: One cause of the Scientific Revolution was a new emphasis on empirical observation and experimentation as the proper basis for gaining knowledge, rather than relying purely on tradition. This shift was driven by figures such as Copernicus, Galileo, and Newton, whose discoveries, like the heliocentric model of the solar system, changed how people understood the natural world.
