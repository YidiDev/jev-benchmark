Q (q01, 3 pts): Describe the political and administrative structure the Mongol Empire used to govern its territory.
A: The Mongol Empire was founded by Genghis Khan, who united the various steppe tribes and then expanded outward really fast. I think what really made this whole thing work was that they didn't need to lean on the old tribal or clan hierarchies that existed before, so like, they could mobilize huge numbers of people from tons of different ethnic backgrounds really quickly and command them without worrying about old loyalties getting in the way. So basically Genghis Khan set it up so that this massive, multi-ethnic population could be organized and moved around fast, which is kind of the whole reason they could conquer so much land so quickly. It really was about being able to command people from all these different backgrounds without the old system getting in the way, over and over that seems to be the key thing here.

Q (q02, 4 pts): Explain the significance of the Indian Ocean trade network before 1500 CE.
A: The Indian Ocean trade network moved a wide variety of goods, including spices, textiles, porcelain, and gold. It connected regions such as East Africa along the Swahili coast, Arabia, India, Southeast Asia, and China. Merchants in these regions exchanged these products over long distances for centuries before 1500. This network linked together many different economies across a huge portion of the eastern hemisphere.

Q (q03, 3 pts): Describe the circumstances of the fall of the Byzantine Empire in 1453.
A: The Ottoman forces brought in massive siege cannons that were able to breach walls that had stood for over a thousand years, and once those walls came down the city could not hold out any longer. This event marked the end of the old Roman imperial line that had continued in the east for centuries. It also handed control over key trade routes in the Eastern Mediterranean to the new rulers, which pushed European merchants and explorers to start looking for alternate routes to Asia instead.

Q (q04, 3 pts): Explain the impact of the Black Death on European society and economy.
A: The Black Death killed about a third of Europe's population in the mid-1300s. It was devastating. People panicked. Many blamed minority groups and scapegoated them. Prayers didn't stop the disease. This caused people to question the Church's authority. Faith in religious leaders was shaken.

Q (q05, 2 pts): Describe how the Aztec Empire extracted resources from the peoples it conquered.
A: The Aztecs used a tribute system. Conquered city-states had to send goods, labor, or soldiers on a regular basis. This tribute funded Tenochtitlan and the ruling elite. The Aztecs did not need to directly govern every conquered area. They just demanded tribute instead.

Q (q06, 3 pts): Explain how the Ottoman Empire used the devshirme system.
A: The devshirme system was a periodic levy of Christian boys taken from the Balkan provinces. These boys were converted to Islam. They were trained for years. Many became elite Janissary soldiers. Others rose to become high administrators in the empire.

Q (q07, 5 pts): Explain the significance of the Atlantic Slave Trade for the development of the Americas.
A: The Atlantic Slave Trade forced millions of enslaved Africans into labor on plantations that produced cash crops like sugar, tobacco, and cotton. The wealth generated by these plantation exports helped build enormous fortunes for European trading empires and fueled colonial economies. Over several centuries, the constant removal of people depopulated and destabilized parts of West and Central Africa. It also left a lasting mark on the Americas through African cultural, religious, and linguistic influences that persist today.

Q (q08, 3 pts): Explain the causes of the Protestant Reformation.
A: In 1517 Martin Luther wrote his Ninety-Five Theses criticizing the Catholic Church, which is usually seen as the spark of the Reformation. His ideas were able to spread quickly across Europe because of the newly invented printing press, which let copies of his writings circulate widely. Some German princes also backed the reform movement for their own political and economic reasons, since breaking with Rome gave them more independence and control over church lands.

Q (q09, 4 pts): Describe the Columbian Exchange and its effects.
A: The Columbian Exchange refers to the transfer of crops, livestock, people, and diseases between the Americas and Afro-Eurasia after 1492. One of the most devastating effects was that Old World diseases like smallpox spread among indigenous populations who had no prior immunity, killing huge numbers of people. At the same time, European livestock and crops introduced to the Americas transformed local ecosystems and diets, and this exchange reshaped societies on both sides of the Atlantic.

Q (q10, 3 pts): Explain the significance of the Tokugawa Shogunate's sakoku policy.
A: The sakoku policy severely restricted foreign trade and travel, essentially closing Japan off from most of the outside world. The Tokugawa Shogunate adopted this policy mainly to curb the spread of Christianity, which they saw as a threat, and to limit the political and economic influence that foreign powers might gain within Japan.

Q (q11, 3 pts): Explain the causes of the Scientific Revolution in Europe.
A: I think one of the big causes of the Scientific Revolution was this new emphasis on empirical observation and experimentation as the basis for knowledge, instead of just relying on old texts. I'm not totally sure of all the details, but I know figures like Copernicus, Galileo, and Newton were really important here, especially with discoveries like heliocentrism which said the Earth revolves around the sun instead of the other way around. It seems like this new way of thinking about the natural world through observation and testing was a pretty big shift for the time.

Q (q12, 4 pts): Explain how Enlightenment political philosophy influenced later revolutions.
A: So Enlightenment philosophy had a lot to do with later revolutions, I think, especially thinkers like Locke, Rousseau, and Montesquieu who wrote a lot about government and how it should work. Their ideas kind of fed into revolutions later on, and I keep thinking about how they directly informed the French Revolution's calls for liberty, equality, and getting rid of absolute monarchy. Like people during the French Revolution were pulling from these same ideas about freedom and equality when they wanted to end the king's total power. So yeah, these thinkers and their writings really shaped the demands people made when they rose up against the monarchy in France.

Q (q13, 5 pts): Describe the causes and effects of the French Revolution.
A: There's a lot going on with the French Revolution honestly. I think it really starts with the fact that the French government was basically drowning in debt, partly from all these expensive wars it had been fighting, and that fiscal crisis put a ton of pressure on the whole system. On top of that you had all this resentment building up over the rigid estates system, where the nobility and the clergy got all these special privileges while everyone else, especially the third estate, was stuck paying most of the taxes and getting none of the benefits. And then there's the Enlightenment ideas floating around too, all this talk about liberty, equality, and popular sovereignty, which gave people a language and a framework to actually push back against the monarchy. Eventually all of this boiled over and the monarchy was overthrown, and Louis XVI was eventually executed, which was obviously a huge deal. But then after that there was just so much instability, with different factions fighting for control, and that chaos is really what opened the door for Napoleon Bonaparte to rise to power in the end. So basically it all connects: debt and inequality and new ideas combined to topple the monarchy, and the mess afterward is what let Napoleon take over.

Q (q14, 4 pts): Explain the significance of the Haitian Revolution.
A: The Haitian Revolution was significant because it was led by Toussaint Louverture, who organized the enslaved population into a powerful uprising against French colonial rule. It stands out as the only successful large-scale slave revolt in history that resulted in the founding of an independent nation, Haiti. France lost what had been its most profitable colony, which weakened its overall position in the Americas. Beyond Haiti itself, the revolution inspired enslaved and colonized peoples elsewhere who saw that liberation was possible, while it also alarmed slaveholding powers who feared similar uprisings in their own territories.

Q (q15, 4 pts): Explain the process of industrialization in Britain.
A: Industrialization in Britain involved a shift away from the old cottage industry system, where people made goods at home, toward centralized factories where production could be done on a much bigger scale. I keep coming back to how Britain had all this coal and iron just sitting there in its own land, abundant domestic coal and iron deposits that could fuel this whole shift toward factories. So really it's this move from home-based work to factories, powered by natural resources that Britain already had access to at home, that seems to be at the heart of how industrialization got going there.

Q (q16, 4 pts): Explain the causes of European imperialism in Africa in the late 19th century.
A: I think European imperialism in Africa was driven by a combination of factors. For one, industrial economies back in Europe needed raw materials and new markets, so I think that pushed them to look at Africa as a place to extract resources and sell goods. There was also, I believe, a lot of competition and rivalry for prestige among the European powers themselves, which got somewhat formalized at events like the Berlin Conference where they divided up territory. On top of that, I think Social Darwinist and racial ideologies were used to justify this domination, framing it as some kind of 'civilizing mission' even though it was really about control and profit. And finally, I'm fairly confident that military technology, like the Maxim gun, gave European forces a huge advantage that made conquest much easier than it would have been otherwise.

Q (q17, 3 pts): Describe the effects of the Opium Wars on China.
A: I think the Opium Wars started because Britain was selling opium into China and China tried to suppress that trade because of all the problems it was causing. I'm not totally sure of every detail, but I know that afterward China was forced into signing unequal treaties, like the Treaty of Nanjing, which made them give up Hong Kong and open up certain treaty ports to foreign control. This seems like a pretty major turning point for China's relationship with foreign powers.

Q (q18, 3 pts): Explain the significance of the Meiji Restoration in Japan.
A: The Meiji Restoration ended the Tokugawa Shogunate and formally restored imperial rule under the emperor. This shift allowed Japan to become a major industrial and military power in a relatively short period of time. As a result, Japan was able to avoid being colonized by Western powers, unlike much of the rest of Asia, and it later went on to project its own imperial power in the region.

Q (q19, 4 pts): Explain the causes of World War I.
A: World War I had a bunch of causes tangled together. One big one was this web of alliances across Europe, so that if two powers got into a conflict, all their allies would basically get dragged in too, and that's a lot of what happened. Then there was the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand in Sarajevo in 1914, which is usually seen as the spark that set everything off. But even before that, there had been this arms race going on for years, including naval competition between Britain and Germany, both building up bigger and bigger fleets and militaries, which just made everyone more ready and willing to go to war when the crisis actually hit. So really it's the alliances plus the assassination plus all that military buildup beforehand that set the stage for the whole thing.

Q (q20, 4 pts): Explain the significance of the Bolshevik Revolution.
A: The Bolshevik Revolution was significant because Vladimir Lenin and the Bolshevik Party seized power in Russia in 1917. Once in control, they pulled Russia out of World War I and began building a Communist state based on their ideology. This created the world's first major Communist state, which both inspired revolutionary movements around the world and alarmed other governments who feared similar uprisings at home.

Q (q21, 4 pts): Describe the causes and consequences of the Great Depression.
A: I think one of the reasons the Great Depression became such a global disaster is that trade and lending between countries were so interconnected by that point, so once the crisis hit it spread rapidly to other economies around the world instead of staying contained. This led to mass unemployment and just really severe hardship for people everywhere, not only in the country where things started. I also think this economic desperation played a role in the rise of authoritarian and fascist movements in several countries, since people were looking for someone who promised to fix things fast.

Q (q22, 4 pts): Explain the causes of World War II in Europe.
A: There's a few things going on with the causes of World War II in Europe. I keep thinking about how resentment over the Treaty of Versailles, like the harsh terms placed after World War I, really fueled a lot of German nationalism in the years after. Then on top of that, the Western powers followed this policy of appeasement, basically trying to avoid conflict by giving in to demands, but that ended up failing to stop the aggression from escalating further. And then the actual trigger for the war breaking out was Germany's invasion of Poland in 1939, which led Britain and France to declare war. So it's kind of this combination of leftover resentment from after the first war, failed appeasement, and then the invasion of Poland that set the whole thing off.

Q (q23, 3 pts): Describe the process of decolonization in Africa after World War II.
A: I think decolonization in Africa happened mainly because the European colonial powers came out of World War II economically and militarily weakened, which made it a lot harder for them to keep holding onto their colonies by force. At the same time, there were rising nationalist movements across Africa, with local leaders organizing their populations to demand self-rule and independence. Because of these combined pressures, most African nations ended up gaining their independence in a wave that was concentrated mainly during the 1950s and 1960s.

Q (q25, 3 pts): Explain the causes of the Chinese Communist Revolution.
A: The Chinese Communist Revolution grew out of the long conflict between the Chinese Communist Party, led by Mao Zedong, and the Nationalist Guomindang government led by Chiang Kai-shek. One major reason the Communists were able to gain support was that they promised land reform, which strongly appealed to the rural peasantry who made up the vast majority of the population.

Q (q26, 2 pts): Explain the significance of the Green Revolution in the 20th century.
A: The Green Revolution refers to the development and spread of high-yield crop varieties along with modern agricultural techniques like fertilizers and irrigation. Its significance lies in how it substantially increased food production, especially in developing nations, which greatly reduced the risk of famine in many parts of the world.

Q (q27, 4 pts): Describe the causes and effects of the fall of the Soviet Union.
A: I think the fall of the Soviet Union had a lot to do with Mikhail Gorbachev's policies of glasnost, or openness, and perestroika, which was about restructuring the economy and government. These reforms seem to have opened the door for more criticism and unrest. At the same time, there were rising nationalist movements within various Soviet republics that pushed hard for independence from Moscow. I believe the end result of all this was that the Cold War ended along with the whole bipolar structure of global power that had existed for decades.

It kept spreading from place to place, country to country, with people demanding more democracy and an end to the way things had been run for so long.
