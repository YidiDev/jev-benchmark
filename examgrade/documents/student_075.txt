Q (q01, 3 pts): Describe the political and administrative structure the Mongol Empire used to govern its territory.
A: I think the Mongol Empire, which was founded by Genghis Khan, used a pretty clever administrative system to control such a huge amount of territory. From what I remember, they organized the population into decimal units, like groups of 10, 100, 1,000, and 10,000 people, and I believe this was mainly for military purposes but it also structured society more broadly. I'm not totally sure, but I think the point of this was that it cut across the old tribal and clan loyalties, so people were loyal to their unit and ultimately to the khan rather than to their birth clan. This probably helped them mobilize and command a massive multi-ethnic population really quickly without having to rely on all the old tribal hierarchies that existed before Genghis Khan unified everyone.

Q (q02, 4 pts): Explain the significance of the Indian Ocean trade network before 1500 CE.
A: The Indian Ocean trade network connected a huge number of regions before 1500. It linked East Africa, particularly the Swahili coast, with Arabia, India, Southeast Asia, and China. This made it one of the largest and most interconnected trading systems in the pre-modern world. Sailors were able to take advantage of the monsoon winds, which reversed direction predictably depending on the season. This allowed merchants to plan round trip voyages, sailing one direction during one part of the year and returning when the winds shifted back.

Q (q03, 3 pts): Describe the circumstances of the fall of the Byzantine Empire in 1453.
A: Constantinople fell in 1453. Ottomans took it. Mehmed II led the siege. He used huge cannons. These cannons broke through the old walls. The walls had stood for centuries before that. The city couldn't hold out against that kind of firepower.

Q (q04, 3 pts): Explain the impact of the Black Death on European society and economy.
A: Okay so the Black Death, I keep thinking about how it hit Europe so hard economically and socially. One big thing is that because so many workers died, there weren't enough laborers left, so the ones who survived could demand higher wages. This kind of broke down the whole manorial and serfdom system because lords needed workers so badly that peasants had way more bargaining power than before, and that ended up speeding along the decline of feudalism, which is kind of a big deal looking back at it. Also, like, the plague caused a huge religious crisis because people prayed and prayed and the disease kept spreading anyway, so people started questioning the Church's authority and whether God was even listening, and this led to a lot of scapegoating, especially of Jewish communities who got blamed for causing it. So yeah, the labor shortage thing and the religious crisis thing, those are the two things I keep coming back to when I think about the effects.

Q (q07, 5 pts): Explain the significance of the Atlantic Slave Trade for the development of the Americas.
A: The Atlantic Slave Trade is honestly such a huge topic, but I keep coming back to how it created this triangular trade system that connected Europe, West Africa, and the Americas together, and through that system millions of enslaved Africans were forcibly transported across the ocean. Once they got to the Americas these people were forced into labor on plantations, growing cash crops like sugar and tobacco and cotton, which were hugely profitable crops for the colonial economies. But also I think it's important that this had a massive negative impact back in Africa too, because over the centuries it depopulated and destabilized parts of West and Central Africa, pulling out so many people and causing conflict as different groups raided each other for captives to sell. And then on the other side of the ocean, this whole process left a lasting mark because African cultural, religious, and linguistic traditions ended up blending into and influencing societies all across the Americas, so you can still see that legacy today in music, religion, language, all sorts of things.

Q (q09, 4 pts): Describe the Columbian Exchange and its effects.
A: I think the Columbian Exchange refers to the big transfer of crops, animals, people, and unfortunately diseases between the Americas and Afro-Eurasia after Columbus's voyages in 1492. One of the most devastating effects, I believe, was that Old World diseases like smallpox spread to indigenous populations in the Americas who had never been exposed before, so they had no immunity and it wiped out huge numbers of people. On kind of the flip side of that, I think new crops from the Americas, like maize and potatoes, made their way to Afro-Eurasia and really helped boost food supplies there, which then contributed to population growth in places like Europe and Asia. I'm not 100% sure about all the finer details, but that's my understanding of the main exchange and its effects.

Q (q11, 3 pts): Explain the causes of the Scientific Revolution in Europe.
A: I think one of the main causes of the Scientific Revolution was that people started challenging the old reliance on classical texts, like the works of Aristotle, and also questioning religious doctrine as the only source of truth about the natural world. This was a pretty big shift because before, most explanations came from ancient authorities or the Church. I believe figures like Copernicus, Galileo, and Newton were central to this, with things like heliocentrism, the idea that the earth revolves around the sun, really shaking up the old worldview that had been accepted for so long.

French revolutionaries also used these ideas. They called for liberty and equality. They wanted to end absolute monarchy. Both revolutions drew directly on this Enlightenment thinking.
