Q (q01, 3 pts): Describe the political and administrative structure the Mongol Empire used to govern its territory.
A: The Mongol Empire was founded by Genghis Khan, who united the Mongol tribes and then expanded outward across Eurasia. One of the key things about how the empire worked was that it let the Mongols rapidly mobilize and command a huge multi-ethnic population made up of many different conquered peoples, without having to rely on the old pre-existing tribal or clan hierarchies that had divided the steppe before. This meant loyalty was owed more directly to the khan and the empire itself rather than to smaller local groups, which helped them move armies and resources quickly across such a massive territory.

Q (q02, 4 pts): Explain the significance of the Indian Ocean trade network before 1500 CE.
A: Indian Ocean trade connected East Africa, the Swahili coast, Arabia, India, Southeast Asia, and China. Sailors used monsoon winds. The winds reversed seasonally. This let them plan round trips reliably. Islam spread along this network too. It spread to the East African coast. It spread to Southeast Asia. Merchants and travelers carried it with them as they traded.

Q (q03, 3 pts): Describe the circumstances of the fall of the Byzantine Empire in 1453.
A: So the fall of Byzantium, I mean it's really the Ottomans under Mehmed II who finally take Constantinople in 1453, and honestly this had been building for a long time but it's Mehmed who actually pulls it off. What made it possible, or at least what really broke the city, was those massive siege cannons the Ottomans built, because Constantinople's walls had held for like a thousand years against basically everyone, but these huge cannons were finally able to blast through those ancient walls. And once the walls fell the city fell, and that was basically it, that was the end of the Roman Empire's imperial line going all the way back to Rome, which is kind of a huge deal when you think about it. And also, since the Ottomans now controlled Constantinople, they controlled a huge chunk of the key trade routes in the Eastern Mediterranean, so Europeans who wanted to trade with Asia had to look for other ways, which is part of why they started looking for new sea routes, so it kind of ties into the Age of Exploration too.

Q (q04, 3 pts): Explain the impact of the Black Death on European society and economy.
A: The Black Death hit Europe in the mid-1300s and it's kind of insane how much death there was, like it killed around a third of the entire population of Europe, which is just a massive number of people gone in a pretty short time. And because so many people died, especially peasant workers, there was suddenly a big labor shortage, so the workers who were left could demand higher wages since lords needed people to work the land. This ended up weakening the whole manorial system and serfdom because lords couldn't force people to stay tied to the land as easily anymore, so it kind of sped up the decline of feudalism in general.

Q (q05, 2 pts): Describe how the Aztec Empire extracted resources from the peoples it conquered.
A: The Aztecs used a tribute system. Conquered city-states had to pay up. They sent goods. They sent labor. They sent soldiers sometimes too. This tribute funded Tenochtitlan. It supported the elite class. The Aztecs didn't need to directly run every conquered area. They just demanded tribute instead.

Q (q06, 3 pts): Explain how the Ottoman Empire used the devshirme system.
A: The devshirme was a periodic levy. Ottomans took Christian boys. They came from Balkan provinces. These boys were converted to Islam. They were trained hard. Many became Janissary soldiers. Others rose to high administrative positions.

Q (q10, 3 pts): Explain the significance of the Tokugawa Shogunate's sakoku policy.
A: I think sakoku basically meant Japan closed itself off from most of the outside world under the Tokugawa Shogunate, restricting foreign trade and travel pretty severely. Q (q11, 3 pts): Explain the causes of the Scientific Revolution in Europe.
A: The Scientific Revolution was driven largely by a new emphasis on empirical observation and experimentation as the basis for knowledge. This approach challenged the older reliance on classical texts, especially Aristotle, and on religious doctrine as the sole sources of truth. Figures like Copernicus, Galileo, and Newton contributed key discoveries, such as heliocentrism, that reinforced this new scientific method and further undermined traditional authorities.

Q (q12, 4 pts): Explain how Enlightenment political philosophy influenced later revolutions.
A: Enlightenment thinkers developed ideas like natural rights, the social contract, and separation of powers. Thinkers such as Locke, Rousseau, and Montesquieu were central to spreading these concepts across Europe. These ideas directly informed the French Revolution, fueling its calls for liberty, equality, and an end to absolute monarchy.

Q (q13, 5 pts): Describe the causes and effects of the French Revolution.
A: Okay so the French Revolution, there's a lot going on here but I'll try to get through it. First there was this chronic government debt problem, France's finances were a mess partly because of all the costly wars they'd been fighting, and that fiscal crisis just kept getting worse. On top of that there was a lot of resentment about the rigid estates system, like the nobility and clergy had all these privileges and didn't pay taxes while everyone else struggled, so that built up a lot of anger among the Third Estate. And then you had Enlightenment ideas floating around about liberty, equality, and popular sovereignty, which gave people a framework to actually question why the monarchy and the whole social structure should even exist in that form. All this combined led to the monarchy getting overthrown, and eventually Louis XVI himself was executed, which was a huge shock to the rest of Europe. But then after the monarchy fell there was just so much instability, with different factions fighting for control, and that chaos is really what opened the door for Napoleon Bonaparte to rise to power and eventually take over.

Q (q14, 4 pts): Explain the significance of the Haitian Revolution.
A: So the Haitian Revolution, I keep thinking about how Toussaint Louverture was the one who really led the uprising of the enslaved population there, which is a huge deal in itself. And what makes it stand out so much is that it was the only successful large-scale slave revolt that actually led to founding an independent nation, which became Haiti. Like there had been slave revolts before but none of them turned into an actual independent country like this one did. And because of that success, it inspired enslaved people and colonized people elsewhere who saw that it was possible to overthrow their oppressors, but at the same time it really alarmed slaveholding powers everywhere, since they were terrified something similar could happen in their own colonies.

Q (q17, 3 pts): Describe the effects of the Opium Wars on China.
A: The Opium Wars stemmed from Britain's opium trade with China and China's efforts to suppress it, since the opium was causing serious social and economic problems. China was militarily defeated by Britain, and later by other Western powers as well. As a result, China was forced to sign unequal treaties, such as the Treaty of Nanjing, which required ceding Hong Kong to Britain and opening several treaty ports to foreign control and trade.

Q (q19, 4 pts): Explain the causes of World War I.
A: A major cause of World War I was the complex web of alliances across Europe, which meant a conflict between just two powers could quickly draw in the rest of the continent. Competing nationalist and imperial ambitions also created ongoing tension among the great powers, as they jockeyed for territory and influence. The immediate trigger was the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand in Sarajevo in 1914. Additionally, a prewar arms race, including intense naval competition between Britain and Germany, had already heightened everyone's readiness and willingness to go to war.
