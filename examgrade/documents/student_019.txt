Q (q01, 3 pts): Describe the political and administrative structure the Mongol Empire used to govern its territory.
A: The Mongol Empire was founded by Genghis Khan, who united the Mongol tribes and led the initial conquests that created the empire. Under his leadership the Mongols expanded rapidly across Asia and into Europe, becoming one of the largest land empires in history.

Q (q02, 4 pts): Explain the significance of the Indian Ocean trade network before 1500 CE.
A: Indian Ocean trade linked East Africa, Arabia, India, Southeast Asia, and China. Monsoon winds reversed seasonally. Sailors used this to plan trips there and back. Islam spread along the Swahili coast and into Southeast Asia. Merchants and travelers carried it with them.

Q (q04, 3 pts): Explain the impact of the Black Death on European society and economy.
A: The Black Death killed about a third of Europe's population in the mid-1300s, which was a massive demographic shock. It also caused religious upheaval, since people turned to scapegoating minority groups like Jews when prayer and religious rituals failed to stop the disease from spreading, and this undermined some people's faith in the Church's authority.

Q (q05, 2 pts): Describe how the Aztec Empire extracted resources from the peoples it conquered.
A: The Aztec Empire used a tribute system where conquered city-states had to regularly send goods, labor, or soldiers to the empire. This meant conquered peoples were not always directly ruled but still had to provide resources on an ongoing basis.

Q (q07, 5 pts): Explain the significance of the Atlantic Slave Trade for the development of the Americas.
A: The Atlantic Slave Trade generated enormous wealth for European trading empires and their colonies through plantation exports. At the same time it depopulated and destabilized large parts of West and Central Africa over the centuries it operated. It also left a lasting African cultural, religious, and linguistic imprint across the Americas that is still visible today.

Q (q09, 4 pts): Describe the Columbian Exchange and its effects.
A: The Columbian Exchange was the transfer of crops, livestock, people, and diseases between the Americas and Afro-Eurasia following Columbus's voyages after 1492. Old World diseases like smallpox devastated indigenous American populations because they had no prior immunity to these illnesses, leading to catastrophic population loss. On the other side, New World crops such as maize and potatoes boosted food supplies in Europe, Asia, and Africa, contributing to significant population growth there. Meanwhile European livestock and crops transformed American ecosystems and diets, fundamentally reshaping societies and environments on both sides of the Atlantic.

Q (q18, 3 pts): Explain the significance of the Meiji Restoration in Japan.
A: The Meiji Restoration ended the Tokugawa Shogunate and formally restored imperial rule in Japan. This marked a major shift in Japanese political leadership after centuries of shogunate control.

And then the actual spark, I believe, was the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand in Sarajevo in 1914, which set everything off.

Q (q21, 4 pts): Describe the causes and consequences of the Great Depression.
A: The Great Depression was triggered by the 1929 U.S. stock market crash. Because global trade and lending were so interconnected, the crisis spread quickly to other economies around the world. This led to mass unemployment and severe hardship for people globally.

Q (q22, 4 pts): Explain the causes of World War II in Europe.

This helped fuel the rise of Hitler and Nazi ideology, which pursued aggressive territorial expansion across Europe. Western powers responded with a policy of appeasement, but this failed to stop Germany's escalating aggression.
