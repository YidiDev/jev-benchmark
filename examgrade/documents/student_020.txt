A: The Mongols set up a decimal system. People got organized into units of 10, 100, 1,000, and 10,000. This cut across old tribal and clan loyalties. It let them mobilize huge numbers of people fast. Q (q05, 2 pts): Describe how the Aztec Empire extracted resources from the peoples it conquered.
A: The Aztec Empire relied on a tribute system. Conquered city-states had to regularly send goods, labor, or soldiers to the empire. This meant subject peoples were constantly providing resources even after being defeated militarily.

Q (q06, 3 pts): Explain how the Ottoman Empire used the devshirme system.
A: I think the devshirme system worked by periodically taking Christian boys, mostly from the Balkan provinces, and bringing them into Ottoman service. From what I remember, these boys were converted to Islam and given training, and a lot of them ended up becoming elite Janissary soldiers or rising into high administrative positions within the empire. I believe the point of this was that it built up a class of soldiers and officials who were personally loyal to the Sultan himself, rather than being tied to hereditary noble families who might have their own competing interests, so it kind of let the Sultan build a power base that answered directly to him.

Q (q07, 5 pts): Explain the significance of the Atlantic Slave Trade for the development of the Americas.
A: The Atlantic Slave Trade linked Europe, West Africa, and the Americas. Millions of enslaved Africans were forcibly shipped across. They were forced to labor on plantations. Crops like sugar, tobacco, and cotton were produced. These exports made huge profits for European trading empires and colonies. Africa lost millions of people over centuries. This depopulated and destabilized parts of West and Central Africa. Enslaved Africans also brought their cultures with them. Their languages, religions, and traditions left lasting marks across the Americas.

Q (q09, 4 pts): Describe the Columbian Exchange and its effects.
