Q (q01, 3 pts): Describe the political and administrative structure the Mongol Empire used to govern its territory.
A: The Mongol Empire was founded by Genghis Khan, who unified the steppe tribes under his leadership. To organize his people and army, he set up a decimal-based system that grouped men into units of 10, 100, 1,000, and 10,000. This structure cut across the older tribal and clan loyalties that had previously divided the Mongols.

Q (q02, 4 pts): Explain the significance of the Indian Ocean trade network before 1500 CE.
A: So the Indian Ocean trade network was basically this huge web connecting East Africa like the Swahili coast, Arabia, India, Southeast Asia, and China, and they were trading things like spices, textiles, porcelain, and gold back and forth constantly. I keep thinking about how it all worked, and I think the big reason it worked so well was the monsoon winds, like they blew one direction part of the year and reversed the other part, so sailors could actually plan when to leave and come back, it wasn't random, they knew the winds would flip and bring them home eventually. So yeah, spices and porcelain and gold moving between Africa, Arabia, India, China, Southeast Asia, all made possible by these predictable monsoon patterns that let merchants time their voyages.

A: The Black Death hit Europe hard. It killed about a third of the population. This happened in the mid 1300s. It was a huge population loss across the continent.

Q (q05, 2 pts): Describe how the Aztec Empire extracted resources from the peoples it conquered.
A: Okay so thinking about how the Aztecs ran their empire, it wasn't really like they sent governors everywhere to directly rule every single conquered city, instead they used this tribute system where the conquered city-states had to regularly hand over stuff, like goods or labor or sometimes soldiers for the military. And this tribute is what basically kept the whole empire running, it funded Tenochtitlan and all the elite class living there, the nobles and priests and rulers, without the Aztecs having to actually administer every single territory they had power over. So it's kind of an indirect system, they let local rulers stay in charge as long as the tribute kept flowing, and that tribute is what paid for the capital and the elites at the top.

Q (q07, 5 pts): Explain the significance of the Atlantic Slave Trade for the development of the Americas.
A: Thinking about the Atlantic Slave Trade and why it mattered so much for the Americas, one big thing is just the wealth, like the plantations growing sugar and tobacco and stuff generated massive profits for European trading empires and for the colonies themselves, that money built up a lot of the colonial economy. Then there's the other side of it, over in Africa, the trade going on for centuries really depopulated and destabilized parts of West and Central Africa, taking away so many people over such a long time really messed with those societies. And then thinking about the Americas again, there's this lasting cultural impact, like African traditions, religions, languages, music, all of that became embedded into American cultures and stayed there long after, so it's this mix of economic wealth for Europe, damage to Africa, and lasting cultural influence in the Americas.

Q (q13, 5 pts): Describe the causes and effects of the French Revolution.

These combined to build pressure against the existing government structure.

Q (q18, 3 pts): Explain the significance of the Meiji Restoration in Japan.
A: The Meiji Restoration led Japan to rapidly modernize its military, industry, and government based on Western models. This transformation happened quickly and touched nearly every part of Japanese society and institutions. As a result, Japan became a major industrial and military power in its own right. This let Japan avoid being colonized by Western powers, and later Japan even began projecting its own imperial power over other nations.

Q (q20, 4 pts): Explain the significance of the Bolshevik Revolution.
A: Vladimir Lenin led the Bolshevik Party to seize power in 1917. They overthrew the provisional government, which had itself replaced the Tsar earlier that same year. After taking power, the Bolsheviks pulled Russia out of World War I. They then began building a new Communist state in its place.

A: The Great Depression began with the 1929 U.S. stock market crash. Because global trade and lending were so interconnected at the time, the crisis quickly spread to other economies around the world. This led to mass unemployment and severe hardship on a global scale. Many people struggled to find work or afford basic necessities during this period.
