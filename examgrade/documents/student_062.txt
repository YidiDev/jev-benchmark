Q (q01, 3 pts): Describe the political and administrative structure the Mongol Empire used to govern its territory.
A: The Mongol Empire was founded by Genghis Khan, who unified the various Mongol and steppe tribes under his rule. To administer his forces and people, he set up a decimal-based system that organized the population into units of 10, 100, 1,000, and 10,000. This structure cut across the old tribal and clan loyalties that had previously divided the steppe peoples, replacing them with a new organizational framework based on these numerical units.

Q (q08, 3 pts): Explain the causes of the Protestant Reformation.
A: One major cause of the Protestant Reformation was widespread objection to the Catholic Church's sale of indulgences and general perceptions of corruption within the Church hierarchy. Many people felt the Church was more focused on wealth and power than on spiritual matters. Another important factor was the invention of the printing press, which allowed Martin Luther's ideas to spread quickly across Europe. Additionally, some German princes supported the reform movement for their own political and economic reasons, since breaking from the Church could increase their independence and wealth.

Q (q09, 4 pts): Describe the Columbian Exchange and its effects.
A: So the Columbian Exchange, I keep thinking about how it went both ways across the Atlantic. On one side, crops from the New World like maize and potatoes ended up boosting food supplies in Afro-Eurasia, which then led to population growth there since people had more reliable food sources. And then going the other way, European livestock and crops came over to the Americas and totally changed the ecosystems there, like animals such as horses and cattle changed how people lived and ate, and this reshaped societies on both sides of the ocean. So basically it's this two-way transformation, new crops helping population grow in the old world, and European stuff transforming the environment and diets in the new world.

Q (q11, 3 pts): Explain the causes of the Scientific Revolution in Europe.
A: One key cause of the Scientific Revolution was a new emphasis on empirical observation and experimentation as the proper basis for gaining knowledge about the natural world. This approach relied on testing ideas through evidence rather than simply accepting inherited beliefs. As a result, this new emphasis challenged the traditional reliance on classical texts, such as those of Aristotle, and on religious doctrine as the sole and final sources of truth about the natural world.
