Q (q01, 3 pts): Describe the political and administrative structure the Mongol Empire used to govern its territory.
A: I think the Mongols organized their people using a decimal system, like breaking everyone into groups of 10, 100, 1,000, and 10,000, and I believe this cut across the older tribal and clan loyalties that used to divide people. I'm not totally sure but I think this was really useful because it let them mobilize and command a huge population made up of lots of different ethnic groups without having to depend on all those old tribal hierarchies that might have been less reliable or loyal.

Q (q02, 4 pts): Explain the significance of the Indian Ocean trade network before 1500 CE.
A: I think the Indian Ocean trade network before 1500 was pretty significant because it connected a huge number of regions like East Africa along the Swahili coast, Arabia, India, Southeast Asia, and China, so it was a really wide-reaching system. Merchants traded things like spices, textiles, porcelain, and gold, which I believe made it possible for a lot of wealth and different goods to circulate. I'm not totally sure but I think the monsoon winds were a big part of why this worked so well, since their seasonal reversal was predictable and let sailors plan trips there and back without just guessing. Also, I think Islam spread along the East African coast and into Southeast Asia partly because of merchants and travelers moving along these routes, so the trade network had religious and cultural effects too, not just economic ones.

Q (q03, 3 pts): Describe the circumstances of the fall of the Byzantine Empire in 1453.
A: Ottomans took Constantinople. Mehmed II led them. Byzantine Empire fell in 1453. This ended Roman imperial line. Ottomans then controlled key trade routes in Eastern Mediterranean. Europeans had to find new routes to Asia because of this.

Q (q04, 3 pts): Explain the impact of the Black Death on European society and economy.
A: I think the Black Death had a huge impact on Europe, killing something like a third of the population in the mid-1300s, which is a pretty staggering number. Because so many workers died, I believe labor became scarce, and this pushed wages up since employers had to compete for fewer available workers, and I think this ended up weakening the whole manorial and serfdom system, kind of speeding along the decline of feudalism. I'm also not totally sure but I think it caused a lot of religious panic too, since people turned to blaming minority groups like Jewish communities for the plague, and there was a broader crisis of faith when prayers and religious rituals didn't stop people from dying, which made some people question the Church's authority.

Q (q05, 2 pts): Describe how the Aztec Empire extracted resources from the peoples it conquered.
A: Aztecs used tribute system. Conquered city-states had to pay goods. Also labor. Also soldiers sometimes. This paid for Tenochtitlan. Supported the elite too. Didn't need to directly rule every place they conquered.

Q (q06, 3 pts): Explain how the Ottoman Empire used the devshirme system.
A: I think the devshirme system was when the Ottomans would periodically take Christian boys from the Balkan provinces, kind of like a levy. I believe these boys were then converted to Islam and trained, and a lot of them ended up becoming elite Janissary soldiers or even rose up to be high administrators in the government. I'm not totally sure but I think the whole point of this was to build a military and administrative class that was personally loyal to the Sultan himself, instead of relying on hereditary nobles who might have their own competing interests or loyalties.

Q (q07, 5 pts): Explain the significance of the Atlantic Slave Trade for the development of the Americas.
A: The Atlantic Slave Trade was significant because it created a triangular trade system linking Europe, West Africa, and the Americas. This system forcibly transported millions of enslaved Africans across the Atlantic under brutal conditions. In the Americas, enslaved people were forced to labor on plantations that produced cash crops like sugar, tobacco, and cotton. These plantation exports generated enormous wealth for European trading empires and shaped colonial economies for centuries. Back in Africa, the trade depopulated and destabilized parts of West and Central Africa over several centuries, disrupting political and social structures there. At the same time, the forced migration produced a lasting African cultural, religious, and linguistic influence across the Americas that is still visible today.

Q (q08, 3 pts): Explain the causes of the Protestant Reformation.
A: Luther posted Ninety-Five Theses in 1517. Criticized the Church. Objected to selling indulgences. Saw Church as corrupt. Printing press spread his ideas fast. German princes backed reform too. Had political and economic reasons.

Q (q09, 4 pts): Describe the Columbian Exchange and its effects.
A: Columbian Exchange happened after 1492. Crops, animals, people, and diseases moved between the Americas and Afro-Eurasia. Smallpox and other Old World diseases wiped out huge numbers of Native people. No immunity existed there. New World crops like maize and potatoes went to Afro-Eurasia. Boosted food supply. Population grew because of it. European livestock and crops changed American ecosystems. Diets changed on both sides. Societies transformed everywhere.

Q (q10, 3 pts): Explain the significance of the Tokugawa Shogunate's sakoku policy.
A: I think the sakoku policy was meant to close Japan off pretty severely from most foreign trade and travel, cutting off a lot of outside contact with the rest of the world. I believe the Shogunate wanted to do this mainly to stop Christianity from spreading further and to limit how much foreign powers could influence Japan's politics and economy. I'm not totally sure but I think they still allowed some limited trade with the Dutch, and this happened through the controlled port of Nagasaki, specifically at a place called Dejima, so it wasn't a complete and total isolation.

Q (q11, 3 pts): Explain the causes of the Scientific Revolution in Europe.
A: New focus on observation. Also experiments. This became the basis for knowledge. Challenged old reliance on classical texts like Aristotle. Also challenged religious doctrine as only source of truth. Copernicus, Galileo, Newton were key figures. Heliocentrism was a major discovery that changed things.

Q (q12, 4 pts): Explain how Enlightenment political philosophy influenced later revolutions.
A: Enlightenment thinkers introduced key political ideas that shaped later revolutions. Concepts like natural rights, the social contract, and separation of powers became central to new political thinking. Thinkers such as Locke, Rousseau, and Montesquieu developed these ideas and spread them widely through their writings. These ideas directly fed into the French Revolution, where people called for liberty, equality, and an end to absolute monarchy based on these Enlightenment principles.

Q (q13, 5 pts): Describe the causes and effects of the French Revolution.
A: I think the French Revolution had a lot of causes, starting with the government being deeply in debt, partly because of expensive wars, which created a serious fiscal crisis. I also believe there was a lot of resentment toward the rigid estates system, since the nobility and clergy had all these privileges while everyone else struggled. Enlightenment ideas about liberty, equality, and popular sovereignty were floating around too, and I think they gave people a new way to imagine government without a king having absolute power. As for effects, I'm pretty sure the monarchy ended up being overthrown, and Louis XVI was eventually executed, which was a huge deal at the time. After that there was a lot of instability, and I think this chaos is part of what eventually allowed Napoleon Bonaparte to rise to power and take control.

Q (q15, 4 pts): Explain the process of industrialization in Britain.
A: So industrialization in Britain, I mean there's a lot going on here, but I think the steam engine is probably the biggest piece of it, since it powered machinery in factories and then later got used for railways and ships too, so it just kept expanding into new areas. And then there's this shift, like people used to work from home doing cottage industry stuff, spinning and weaving in their houses, but that moved into centralized factories where everything got concentrated in one place, which I guess ties back into the steam engine thing since factories needed that power source. Britain also just happened to have a lot of coal and iron sitting there domestically, which fed right into building machines and running steam engines, so that's kind of the resource side of things. And then because of all this factory work concentrating in cities, you get rapid urbanization, tons of workers moving into these industrial cities, and honestly the conditions were pretty harsh from what I remember, overcrowded and rough, so it's like the whole process fed into itself, coal and iron, steam engines, factories, and then cities growing around all of it.

Q (q16, 4 pts): Explain the causes of European imperialism in Africa in the late 19th century.
A: European imperialism in Africa was driven by several interconnected causes. Industrial economies needed raw materials and new markets, so European powers looked to Africa to fuel their factories and sell goods. There was also intense competition and rivalry among European nations for prestige, which got formalized at events like the Berlin Conference where Africa was divided up. Ideologies like Social Darwinism and racial theories were used to justify this domination, often framed as a "civilizing mission" to spread European culture and religion. Finally, military technology such as the Maxim gun gave Europeans a decisive advantage over African resistance, making conquest much easier to carry out.

A: Britain traded opium with China. China tried to stop it. War broke out. China lost militarily to Britain. Later lost to other powers too. Forced to sign unequal treaties. Treaty of Nanjing was one. Had to give up Hong Kong. Had to open treaty ports. Foreigners controlled those ports.

Q (q18, 3 pts): Explain the significance of the Meiji Restoration in Japan.
A: The Meiji Restoration ended the Tokugawa Shogunate and formally restored imperial rule under the emperor. This shift allowed Japan to rapidly modernize its military, industry, and government institutions, largely modeling them on Western nations. As a result of these reforms, Japan became a major industrial and military power in its own right. This let Japan avoid being colonized by Western powers, and it later allowed Japan to project its own imperial power over other parts of Asia.

A: Okay so World War I, there's kind of a lot of pieces here that all connect together. I think one big thing is the alliance system, like all these countries had agreements with each other, so if two powers got into a fight it could just drag everyone else in too, kind of like a chain reaction. And then there's also nationalism and imperial ambitions, all these countries competing for territory and status, which just kept building tension between the great powers over time. Then you've got the actual spark, which I think was the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand in Sarajevo in 1914, that's the event that actually set things off even though the tension had been building already. And I shouldn't forget the arms race beforehand, like Britain and Germany were competing over building up their navies, and I think that kind of arms race just made everyone more on edge and ready to go to war once something happened. So really it's the alliances, the nationalism and rivalry, the assassination, and the arms race all feeding into each other that caused the war.

Q (q20, 4 pts): Explain the significance of the Bolshevik Revolution.
A: The Bolshevik Revolution was significant because Vladimir Lenin and the Bolshevik Party seized power in 1917 in Russia. They overthrew the provisional government, which had itself only recently replaced the Tsar earlier that same year. After taking power, the Bolsheviks pulled Russia out of World War I and began constructing a Communist state. This made Russia the site of the world's first major Communist state, which both inspired revolutionary movements in other countries and alarmed capitalist governments who feared similar uprisings at home.

Q (q21, 4 pts): Describe the causes and consequences of the Great Depression.
A: The Great Depression was triggered by the 1929 U.S. stock market crash, which set off a chain of economic panic. Because global trade and lending were so interconnected at this point, the crisis did not stay contained to the United States and instead spread rapidly to other economies around the world. The widespread economic desperation that followed contributed to the rise of authoritarian and fascist movements in several countries, as people looked for radical solutions to their economic suffering.
