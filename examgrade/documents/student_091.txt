Q (q03, 3 pts): Describe the circumstances of the fall of the Byzantine Empire in 1453.
A: The Ottomans used massive siege cannons to break through Constantinople's ancient walls, which had held for centuries before that. This was a huge deal because it showed how gunpowder weapons were changing warfare. The fall of the city also marked the end of the Roman imperial line, since Byzantium was really the continuation of Rome. It also gave the Ottomans control over key trade routes in the Eastern Mediterranean, which pushed Europeans to look for new routes to Asia instead, eventually leading to exploration like Columbus's voyages.

Q (q06, 3 pts): Explain how the Ottoman Empire used the devshirme system.
A: The devshirme system involved the Ottomans periodically taking Christian boys from provinces in the Balkans. This was a levy that happened regularly rather than a one time thing. Through this practice, the Ottomans built up a military and administrative class that was loyal directly to the Sultan. This mattered because it meant power wasn't tied to hereditary nobles who might have their own competing interests.

Q (q18, 3 pts): Explain the significance of the Meiji Restoration in Japan.
A: The Meiji Restoration led Japan to rapidly modernize its military, industry, and government institutions, largely by copying Western models. This transformation happened remarkably fast compared to other non-Western nations at the time. As a result, Japan became a major industrial and military power in its own right. This allowed Japan to avoid being colonized by Western powers, unlike much of the rest of Asia, and later Japan even began projecting its own imperial power over other nations.
