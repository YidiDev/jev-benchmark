Q (q01, 3 pts): Describe the political and administrative structure the Mongol Empire used to govern its territory.
A: The Mongol Empire was founded by Genghis Khan, who unified the steppe tribes under his rule. To govern and organize his people, he set up a decimal-based military-administrative system, dividing the population into units of 10, 100, 1,000, and 10,000. This structure deliberately cut across older tribal and clan loyalties, breaking down the traditional divisions that had caused infighting. As a result, the Mongols were able to quickly mobilize and command a massive, multi-ethnic population without depending on pre-existing tribal hierarchies, giving them a very efficient and flexible administrative and military machine.

Q (q02, 4 pts): Explain the significance of the Indian Ocean trade network before 1500 CE.
A: The Indian Ocean trade network relied heavily on the monsoon winds, which reversed direction predictably with the seasons. This let sailors plan round trip voyages, since they knew when the winds would blow them one way and then bring them back later in the year. This predictability made long distance sea trade far more reliable than something like crossing open ocean without such patterns. The network was also a major channel for the spread of Islam, as Muslim merchants and travelers carried their faith to places like the East African coast and Southeast Asia, where local populations gradually adopted the religion through contact with these traders.

Q (q05, 2 pts): Describe how the Aztec Empire extracted resources from the peoples it conquered.
A: The Aztec Empire extracted resources mainly through a system of provincial governors who were sent out to distant lands to personally farm the resources themselves and bring them back to Tenochtitlan by canoe. They also relied on a currency system based on printed paper money that circulated across all the city states, which allowed the central government to tax people directly in cash rather than goods. Additionally, the Aztecs built a large standing navy to patrol the coastlines and prevent smuggling of goods out of the empire, which helped keep resources flowing into the capital.
