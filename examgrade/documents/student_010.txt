Q (q03, 3 pts): Describe the circumstances of the fall of the Byzantine Empire in 1453.
A: The Byzantine Empire fell in 1453 when the Ottoman Empire, led by Sultan Mehmed II, captured the capital city of Constantinople. The Ottomans were able to breach the city's ancient and previously impenetrable walls by using massive siege cannons, which was a major military innovation at the time. This conquest marked the definitive end of the Roman imperial line that had continued through Byzantium for centuries. It also gave the Ottomans control over key trade routes in the Eastern Mediterranean, which cut off easy European access to Asian goods and pushed European states to search for new maritime trade routes to Asia.

Q (q04, 3 pts): Explain the impact of the Black Death on European society and economy.
A: The Black Death caused a huge labor shortage in Europe because so many workers died. This drove up wages for the surviving peasants and weakened the manorial system, which sped up the decline of feudalism since lords had less control over labor. The plague also caused a religious crisis because people prayed for relief and it didn't stop the disease, which made many question the Church's authority. It also led to scapegoating of minority groups, like Jewish communities, who were blamed for the plague.

Q (q06, 3 pts): Explain how the Ottoman Empire used the devshirme system.
A: The devshirme system was a periodic levy where the Ottoman Empire took Christian boys from the Balkan provinces of the empire. This practice helped the Sultan build a military and administrative class that was loyal directly to him. Because these people came from outside the traditional hereditary nobility, their loyalty was tied personally to the Sultan rather than to any noble family.

Once they got to the Americas these enslaved people were forced into labor on plantations, growing cash crops like sugar, tobacco, and cotton, which were huge moneymakers. And that plantation labor and those exports generated enormous wealth for European trading empires and for the colonial economies in the Americas, like it really fueled a lot of European economic growth honestly. Also, because of all these enslaved Africans being brought over, there ended up being this lasting African cultural, religious, and linguistic influence all across the Americas, you can see it in music, religion, language, food, all sorts of things even today. So yeah, triangular trade, forced labor, wealth generation, and cultural influence, that's basically the whole picture of why it mattered so much.

Q (q09, 4 pts): Describe the Columbian Exchange and its effects.
A: The Columbian Exchange was the transfer of crops, livestock, people, and diseases between the Americas and Afro-Eurasia after Columbus arrived in 1492. One major effect was that Old World diseases like smallpox devastated indigenous populations in the Americas because they had no prior immunity to these illnesses. On the other hand, European livestock and crops that were introduced transformed American ecosystems and diets. This exchange reshaped societies on both sides of the Atlantic in lasting ways.

Q (q13, 5 pts): Describe the causes and effects of the French Revolution.
A: The French Revolution had several key causes. Chronic government debt, made worse by costly wars like support for the American Revolution, created a severe fiscal crisis for the monarchy. There was also widespread resentment of the rigid estates system, which gave excessive privileges to the nobility and clergy while burdening the common people. Enlightenment ideas about liberty, equality, and popular sovereignty gave revolutionaries a philosophical basis to challenge the old order. As a result of the revolution, the monarchy was overthrown and King Louis XVI was eventually executed. The instability and power vacuum that followed eventually allowed Napoleon Bonaparte to rise to power and take control of France.

Q (q15, 4 pts): Explain the process of industrialization in Britain.
A: Industrialization in Britain relied heavily on the steam engine, which powered machinery in factories and later powered railways and ships. This technology allowed production to shift from home-based cottage industry to centralized factories where goods could be made faster and in larger quantities. As factories grew, there was rapid urbanization because workers moved from rural areas into industrial cities to find jobs. These cities often had harsh and crowded living conditions for the new working class.

Q (q17, 3 pts): Describe the effects of the Opium Wars on China.
A: The Opium Wars stemmed from Britain's opium trade with China and China's efforts to suppress that trade because of the damage it was causing. China was militarily defeated in these conflicts, first by Britain and later by other foreign powers as well. As a result, China was forced to sign unequal treaties, such as the Treaty of Nanjing, which required it to cede Hong Kong to Britain and open several treaty ports to foreign trade and control.

Q (q24, 2 pts): Explain the significance of the Non-Aligned Movement during the Cold War.
A: So the Non-Aligned Movement was basically this group of countries during the Cold War that wanted to stay out of picking sides between the US-led bloc and the Soviet-led bloc, they didn't want to formally align with either superpower even though there was so much pressure to pick one side or the other. Leaders like Jawaharlal Nehru of India, Gamal Abdel Nasser of Egypt, and Josip Broz Tito of Yugoslavia were key figures pushing this idea, trying to carve out an independent path for their countries instead of just becoming a satellite of the US or the USSR. So really the significance is this attempt to stay neutral and independent, led by these prominent leaders from different parts of the world, rather than getting dragged into the bipolar Cold War conflict.
