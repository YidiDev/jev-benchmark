Q (q01, 3 pts): Describe the political and administrative structure the Mongol Empire used to govern its territory.
A: The Mongol Empire was founded by Genghis Khan, who unified the Mongol tribes and built a massive empire through conquest. Rather than governing purely through old tribal or clan loyalties, the empire's structure allowed the Mongols to quickly mobilize soldiers and administer a huge, diverse population across Asia and Europe. This flexibility helped them rule over many different ethnic groups without being tied down to previous local hierarchies.

Q (q02, 4 pts): Explain the significance of the Indian Ocean trade network before 1500 CE.
A: The Indian Ocean trade network connected East Africa along the Swahili coast, Arabia, India, Southeast Asia, and China into one large trading system. Sailors were able to use this network effectively because of the monsoon winds, which reversed direction predictably with the seasons. This meant merchants could plan voyages out and back within a fairly reliable yearly cycle, which made long-distance sea trade much more practical than it otherwise would have been.

Q (q03, 3 pts): Describe the circumstances of the fall of the Byzantine Empire in 1453.
A: The fall of the Byzantine Empire happened in 1453 when the Ottoman Empire, led by Sultan Mehmed II, laid siege to and captured the capital city of Constantinople. The Ottomans were able to break through the city's famously strong ancient walls by using massive siege cannons, which were a relatively new and powerful technology at the time. This bombardment finally allowed Ottoman forces to breach defenses that had held for centuries.

Q (q04, 3 pts): Explain the impact of the Black Death on European society and economy.
A: The Black Death had major effects on Europe's economy and society. Because so many peasants and workers died, there was a serious labor shortage afterward, which gave surviving workers more bargaining power and pushed wages up. This weakened the manorial system and serfdom, since lords could no longer control labor the way they used to, and it helped speed along the decline of feudalism. The plague also caused a religious crisis, since prayer and the Church did not seem to stop the disease, leading people to question Church authority, and it led to scapegoating of minority groups like Jewish communities who were blamed for spreading the sickness.

Q (q08, 3 pts): Explain the causes of the Protestant Reformation.
A: The Protestant Reformation began largely because of Martin Luther, who in 1517 wrote his Ninety-Five Theses attacking Church practices. A major target of his criticism was the sale of indulgences, along with broader corruption he saw within the Catholic Church, which he felt betrayed true Christian teaching. His ideas were able to spread extremely fast because of the printing press, which made pamphlets and translated texts widely available. Additionally, some German princes chose to support the reform movement for their own political and economic reasons, such as gaining independence from imperial and papal authority.

Q (q14, 4 pts): Explain the significance of the Haitian Revolution.
A: The Haitian Revolution was led by Toussaint Louverture, who guided the enslaved population's uprising against French colonial rule. It stands out as the only successful large-scale slave revolt in history that resulted in the founding of an independent nation, Haiti. As a result of the revolution, France lost what had been its most profitable colony, which weakened its overall position and influence in the Americas. The revolution also inspired enslaved and colonized people elsewhere to imagine similar uprisings, while it deeply alarmed other slaveholding powers who feared similar revolts in their own territories.

Q (q15, 4 pts): Explain the process of industrialization in Britain.
A: Industrialization in Britain was driven significantly by the steam engine, which was used to power new machinery and later railways and steamships. This technology helped enable a shift away from home-based cottage industry toward centralized factories where production could be organized on a much larger scale. Britain also had abundant domestic coal and iron deposits, which provided the raw materials and energy needed to fuel this industrial growth. As factories multiplied, rapid urbanization occurred as workers moved from rural areas into growing industrial cities, often facing harsh and difficult living and working conditions.

Q (q16, 4 pts): Explain the causes of European imperialism in Africa in the late 19th century.
A: European imperialism in Africa was driven partly by the desire for raw materials and new markets to support growing industrial economies back home. There was also intense competition and rivalry for prestige among European powers, which was formalized through events like the Berlin Conference that divided up African territory. Social Darwinist and racial ideologies were used to justify this domination, framing colonization as a supposed "civilizing mission." Finally, superior military technology, such as the Maxim gun, gave European powers a decisive advantage that made rapid conquest possible.

Q (q18, 3 pts): Explain the significance of the Meiji Restoration in Japan.
A: The Meiji Restoration led Japan to rapidly modernize its military, industry, and government institutions, largely by studying and adopting Western models. This transformation allowed Japan to become a major industrial and military power in a relatively short time. As a result, Japan was able to avoid the colonization that affected much of the rest of Asia, and it later even began projecting its own imperial power onto neighboring regions.
