Q (q01, 3 pts): Describe the political and administrative structure the Mongol Empire used to govern its territory.
A: The Mongols governed by organizing their empire in a way that let them command huge numbers of different peoples without needing to rely on the old tribal or clan systems that existed before. This meant they could quickly mobilize soldiers and administrators from many ethnic groups and move fast across their territory. It was a system built for speed and control over a massive and diverse population.

Q (q02, 4 pts): Explain the significance of the Indian Ocean trade network before 1500 CE.
A: The Indian Ocean trade network connected East Africa, particularly the Swahili coast, with Arabia, India, Southeast Asia, and China. This constant contact between merchants and travelers meant that Islam spread along the East African coast and into Southeast Asia as traders brought their religion with them. Because of this network, distant regions of the world became linked culturally as well as economically, with religion moving alongside people.

Q (q04, 3 pts): Explain the impact of the Black Death on European society and economy.
A: I think the Black Death killed something like a third of the population of Europe back in the mid 1300s, which is honestly kind of hard to imagine happening today. Because so many people died, I believe there weren't enough workers left afterward, so laborers could demand higher wages, and this is supposed to have really hurt the whole manorial and serfdom system that had held Europe together for so long. I'm not totally sure but I think this is often described as speeding up the decline of feudalism since lords lost a lot of their control over peasants.

Q (q05, 2 pts): Describe how the Aztec Empire extracted resources from the peoples it conquered.
A: The Aztecs used tribute. Conquered city-states had to pay up. They gave goods. They gave labor. Sometimes soldiers too. This paid for Tenochtitlan. It supported the elite. The Aztecs didn't need to run every region directly. Local rulers stayed in charge. Tribute just flowed to the top. Simple system. Effective system.

Q (q06, 3 pts): Explain how the Ottoman Empire used the devshirme system.
A: The devshirme system was when the Ottomans took Christian boys periodically from their Balkan provinces. This gave the Sultan a group of people who owed loyalty directly to him instead of to any noble family, so it helped build a military and administrative class that was tied to him personally. It let the Sultan avoid depending on hereditary nobles who might have their own competing interests.

Q (q07, 5 pts): Explain the significance of the Atlantic Slave Trade for the development of the Americas.
A: So thinking about the Atlantic Slave Trade and how it shaped the Americas, I keep coming back to the fact that the plantations, like sugar and other cash crops, made huge amounts of money for European trading empires and for the colonies themselves, so economically it was just enormous, like this whole system generated wealth on a scale that's hard to really picture. But then also I have to remember it wasn't just about the Americas getting rich, because over in Africa, West and Central Africa specifically, this trade going on for centuries really tore apart societies, depopulated regions, and generally destabilized things there, so it's not like Africa was untouched, it was deeply harmed. And then coming back around to the Americas side again, all these enslaved people brought their culture with them, so you get African religious practices, languages, music, all sorts of things blending into and shaping American societies in ways that are still visible today. So overall it's this three part thing, wealth for Europe and the colonies, devastation in Africa, and lasting cultural influence in the Americas.

Q (q08, 3 pts): Explain the causes of the Protestant Reformation.
A: People were mad at the church. Selling indulgences. Seen as corrupt. That was one cause. Also the printing press existed. Luther's ideas spread fast because of it. German princes liked reform too. They had political and money reasons to back it. Those two things combined.

Q (q09, 4 pts): Describe the Columbian Exchange and its effects.
A: Okay so the Columbian Exchange, I keep thinking about how it went both ways across the ocean. On one side, crops from the New World like maize and potatoes made it over to Afro-Eurasia and this actually boosted food supplies there, which then let populations grow more than before, so that's a pretty big deal for places like Europe and Asia. And then going the other way, European livestock and crops came into the Americas and this really transformed the ecosystems there, changed diets, changed how people lived, basically reshaped societies on both sides of the ocean in different ways. So it's kind of a two way street of transformation, both places ended up looking pretty different because of what got exchanged.

Q (q11, 3 pts): Explain the causes of the Scientific Revolution in Europe.

Q (q13, 5 pts): Describe the causes and effects of the French Revolution.

These events marked a dramatic break from the old system of rule in France.

Q (q18, 3 pts): Explain the significance of the Meiji Restoration in Japan.
A: The Meiji Restoration led Japan to rapidly modernize its military, its industries, and its government institutions, largely by copying models from Western nations. Because of this rapid transformation, Japan became a major industrial and military power in its own right. This let Japan avoid being colonized like much of the rest of Asia, and it later allowed Japan to project its own imperial power onto other nations.

Q (q19, 4 pts): Explain the causes of World War I.
A: Thinking about what caused World War I, I keep coming back to how tangled the alliance systems were, because a conflict between just two countries could pull basically all of Europe into war since everyone was bound by these agreements to defend each other. And then there's also just the general tension building up from nationalist and imperial rivalries, like countries competing for status, territory, and influence over each other, which created this underlying friction that never really went away. On top of all that there was this arms race happening before the war, especially the naval buildup between Britain and Germany, both trying to outbuild each other's fleets, and that kind of competition made everyone more ready and willing to actually go to war when the moment came. So really it's alliances, nationalism and imperial competition, and the arms race all feeding into each other.

A: The Bolsheviks overthrew the provisional government, which had itself replaced the Tsar earlier in 1917. This created the world's first major Communist state. It ended up inspiring revolutionary movements around the world while also alarming other governments who feared the spread of communism.

Q (q22, 4 pts): Explain the causes of World War II in Europe.

Eventually Germany invaded Poland in 1939, and that's what finally triggered Britain and France to declare war, so it all builds from resentment over Versailles, to Hitler's rise and expansionism, to failed appeasement, to the actual invasion that set off the war.

Q (q24, 2 pts): Explain the significance of the Non-Aligned Movement during the Cold War.
A: Thinking about the Non-Aligned Movement, I think the whole point was that these countries wanted to stay out of the Cold War rivalry, so they tried not to formally side with either the US-led bloc or the Soviet-led bloc, kind of carving out their own independent path instead of picking a side. Leaders like Jawaharlal Nehru of India, Gamal Abdel Nasser of Egypt, and Josip Broz Tito of Yugoslavia were some of the big figures pushing this idea, trying to build solidarity among newly independent and developing nations rather than getting pulled into the superpower conflict. So really it was about carving out a third path outside the two blocs, led by these prominent figures from different parts of the world.
