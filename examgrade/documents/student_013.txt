Q (q05, 2 pts): Describe how the Aztec Empire extracted resources from the peoples it conquered.
A: So the Aztec Empire, I think, mainly extracted resources through this tribute system, like they didn't really conquer places and then run them directly, instead they made the conquered city-states pay tribute on a regular basis. And this tribute could be goods, or labor, or sometimes military service when needed, so it was kind of flexible depending on what the empire needed at the time. And I keep coming back to this idea that this let them fund Tenochtitlan, the capital, and support the elite class there, without having to send administrators everywhere to directly govern every single conquered territory. So basically instead of running the whole empire hands-on, they just made local rulers keep sending stuff, tribute, labor, soldiers, whatever, back to the center, and that's how the wealth and power of the empire and capital was maintained without a huge bureaucracy managing every conquered place directly.

Q (q09, 4 pts): Describe the Columbian Exchange and its effects.
A: After 1492, the Columbian Exchange involved crops, livestock, people, and diseases moving between the Americas and Afro-Eurasia. Old World diseases such as smallpox devastated indigenous populations in the Americas because they had no prior immunity to these illnesses. On the other hand, New World crops like maize and potatoes were brought to Afro-Eurasia, and they boosted food supplies there, which helped populations grow. European livestock and crops were also introduced to the Americas, and this transformed ecosystems and diets there. Overall, this exchange reshaped societies on both sides of the Atlantic in major ways.

Q (q24, 2 pts): Explain the significance of the Non-Aligned Movement during the Cold War.
A: The Non-Aligned Movement was significant because its member states chose not to formally align with either the U.S.-led bloc or the Soviet-led bloc during the Cold War. This allowed countries to maintain a degree of independence in foreign policy rather than being forced to pick a side in the broader superpower rivalry. Leaders such as Jawaharlal Nehru of India, Gamal Abdel Nasser of Egypt, and Josip Broz Tito of Yugoslavia were key figures associated with this movement. Together, they represented an effort by newly independent and developing nations to chart their own path outside the bipolar Cold War structure.
