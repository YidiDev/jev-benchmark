Q (q01, 3 pts): Describe the political and administrative structure the Mongol Empire used to govern its territory.
A: Okay so the Mongol Empire, which was founded by Genghis Khan, basically ran on this decimal system for organizing people, like units of 10, then 100, then 1,000, then 10,000, and that's how they set up their whole military and administrative structure. It's kind of interesting because instead of keeping the old tribal and clan groups together, which is what everyone had been doing forever, they mixed people up into these decimal units, so old loyalties to your tribe or clan got cut across and broken down. And I keep coming back to why this mattered, but basically because of this system Genghis Khan and the leaders after him could mobilize just a massive multi-ethnic population really fast without having to depend on all these pre-existing tribal hierarchies that might compete with the khan's authority. So really the decimal system, units of 10 up to 10,000, cutting across tribal lines, that was the core of how the Mongols governed and organized such a huge empire.

Q (q02, 4 pts): Explain the significance of the Indian Ocean trade network before 1500 CE.
A: The Indian Ocean trade network before 1500 was significant for several reasons. First, it moved a wide variety of goods including spices, textiles, porcelain, and gold across long distances. Second, it connected a huge range of regions, linking East Africa along the Swahili coast with Arabia, India, Southeast Asia, and China. Third, this trade was made possible by the monsoon winds, which reversed direction predictably with the seasons, so sailors could time their voyages out and back reliably. Finally, this network was also a vehicle for cultural and religious exchange, most notably the spread of Islam along the East African coast and into Southeast Asia through the traveling merchants who used these routes.

Q (q03, 3 pts): Describe the circumstances of the fall of the Byzantine Empire in 1453.
A: The fall of the Byzantine Empire in 1453 came when the Ottoman Empire, led by Mehmed II, captured the capital city of Constantinople. The Ottomans were able to break through the city's ancient and famously strong walls by using massive siege cannons, which had not really been used on that scale before. This conquest marked the definitive end of the Roman imperial line, since Byzantium had been considered the continuation of Rome. It also gave the Ottomans control over key trade routes in the Eastern Mediterranean, which cut Europeans off from easy access to Asian goods and pushed them to search for alternative sea routes to Asia.

Q (q07, 5 pts): Explain the significance of the Atlantic Slave Trade for the development of the Americas.
A: The Atlantic Slave Trade was hugely significant for the development of the Americas in multiple ways. First, it was part of a triangular trade system linking Europe, West Africa, and the Americas, through which millions of enslaved Africans were forcibly transported across the Atlantic. Second, these enslaved people were forced into brutal labor on plantations that produced cash crops like sugar, tobacco, and cotton. Third, the wealth generated from these plantation exports was enormous and helped enrich European trading empires as well as the colonial economies in the Americas. Fourth, this trade depopulated and destabilized parts of West and Central Africa over the centuries it operated, disrupting societies and political structures there. Finally, the forced migration of Africans left a lasting legacy of African cultural, religious, and linguistic influence throughout the Americas that persists today.
