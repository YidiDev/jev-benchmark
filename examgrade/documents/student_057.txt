Q (q02, 4 pts): Explain the significance of the Indian Ocean trade network before 1500 CE.
A: So the Indian Ocean trade network, I think what made it significant was just how many different kinds of goods were moving around, like spices and textiles and porcelain and gold, all getting traded back and forth across the ocean. And then there's the monsoon winds thing, which I keep coming back to because it's kind of the key to the whole system working, since the winds would blow one direction part of the year and then reverse later, so sailors could actually plan out a round trip instead of just going one way and being stuck. That predictability of the monsoons really let trade become consistent and repeatable instead of random. Also I remember that Islam spread along places like the East African coast and into Southeast Asia because of merchants and travelers moving along these trade routes, spreading their religion as they went through business and everyday contact. So overall it's the goods, the monsoon winds, and the religious spread that I think mattered most here.

Q (q09, 4 pts): Describe the Columbian Exchange and its effects.
A: The Columbian Exchange, I think, was basically this huge transfer of stuff after 1492 between the Americas and Afro-Eurasia, like crops, animals, people, and unfortunately diseases too, all going back and forth across the ocean. It's kind of amazing how much moved in both directions honestly. One of the biggest effects, and I keep thinking about this one, is that Old World diseases like smallpox just devastated Native American populations because they had no immunity to these new diseases at all, so it caused massive death tolls. And then on the flip side, European livestock and crops came over and totally reshaped American ecosystems, changing diets and how land was used, and this transformation went both ways, reshaping societies in the Americas and in Afro-Eurasia too. So overall it's this exchange of crops, animals, and disease, and the devastating impact of disease, plus the ecological reshaping, that I think are the core parts of this.

Q (q14, 4 pts): Explain the significance of the Haitian Revolution.
A: I think the Haitian Revolution was really significant for a bunch of reasons, though I'm not totally sure I'll get every detail perfect. Toussaint Louverture led the enslaved population in their uprising against French colonial rule, and I believe this ended up being the only successful large-scale slave revolt in history that actually led to the creation of an independent nation, which became Haiti. Because of this, France lost what had been its most profitable colony, which I think really weakened France's overall position and wealth in the Americas. I also think this revolution had a big ripple effect beyond Haiti itself, inspiring enslaved and colonized people elsewhere who saw that a successful revolt against slavery and colonial power was actually possible, while at the same time it alarmed other slaveholding powers who worried similar revolts might happen in their own territories. So overall I'd say it's significant both for what it directly achieved in Haiti and for the broader fear and inspiration it created around the Atlantic world.
