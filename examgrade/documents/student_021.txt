Q (q05, 2 pts): Describe how the Aztec Empire extracted resources from the peoples it conquered.
A: The Aztecs used a tribute system. Conquered city-states had to send goods, labor, or soldiers. This funded Tenochtitlan and the elite. They didn't need to directly govern every conquered area. It kept the empire running without full administration.

Q (q06, 3 pts): Explain how the Ottoman Empire used the devshirme system.
A: The devshirme system was a periodic levy where the Ottomans took Christian boys from Balkan provinces. These boys were converted to Islam and trained. Many became elite Janissary soldiers, while others rose to high administrative positions. This system was significant because it built a loyal military and bureaucratic class tied personally to the Sultan, rather than to hereditary noble families who might challenge his authority.

Q (q07, 5 pts): Explain the significance of the Atlantic Slave Trade for the development of the Americas.
A: The Atlantic Slave Trade was built around a triangular trade system connecting Europe, West Africa, and the Americas. Millions of enslaved Africans were forcibly transported across the Atlantic to work on plantations. These enslaved people provided forced labor for cash crops like sugar, tobacco, and cotton. The wealth generated by these plantation exports enriched European trading empires and colonial economies enormously. At the same time, the slave trade depopulated and destabilized parts of West and Central Africa over centuries. It also left a lasting legacy of African cultural, religious, and linguistic influence throughout the Americas.

Q (q09, 4 pts): Describe the Columbian Exchange and its effects.
A: The Columbian Exchange refers to the transfer of crops, livestock, people, and diseases between the Americas and Afro-Eurasia after 1492. Old World diseases like smallpox devastated indigenous American populations who had no immunity to them. In turn, New World crops like maize and potatoes boosted food supplies and population growth across Afro-Eurasia. European livestock and crops also transformed American ecosystems and diets, reshaping societies on both sides of the Atlantic.

Q (q10, 3 pts): Explain the significance of the Tokugawa Shogunate's sakoku policy.
A: Sakoku severely restricted foreign trade and travel, essentially closing Japan off from most of the outside world. The Tokugawa Shogunate implemented this policy to curb the spread of Christianity and limit foreign political and economic influence over Japan. However, it was not a total closure, since limited Dutch trade was still permitted through the controlled port of Nagasaki at Dejima.

Q (q12, 4 pts): Explain how Enlightenment political philosophy influenced later revolutions.
A: Enlightenment philosophy introduced ideas like natural rights, the social contract, and separation of powers. Thinkers such as Locke, Rousseau, and Montesquieu developed these concepts. Locke's ideas about natural rights and government by consent directly informed the American Revolution's arguments for self-government and independence from Britain. Rousseau's and Montesquieu's ideas about popular sovereignty and separation of powers also directly informed the French Revolution's calls for liberty, equality, and an end to absolute monarchy.

Q (q16, 4 pts): Explain the causes of European imperialism in Africa in the late 19th century.
A: European imperialism in Africa was driven by the desire for raw materials and new markets to fuel industrial economies back home. There was also intense competition and prestige rivalry among European powers, which was formalized at events like the Berlin Conference where Africa was divided up. Social Darwinist and racial ideologies were used to justify this domination as a supposed 'civilizing mission.' Finally, superior military technology, such as the Maxim gun, gave Europeans a decisive advantage over African resistance.

Q (q17, 3 pts): Describe the effects of the Opium Wars on China.
A: Britain was trading opium into China, and China tried to suppress this trade because of its harmful effects. This led to military conflict, and China was decisively defeated by Britain, and later by other powers as well. As a result, China was forced to sign unequal treaties, such as the Treaty of Nanjing, which ceded Hong Kong to Britain and opened treaty ports to foreign control.

Q (q22, 4 pts): Explain the causes of World War II in Europe.
A: Resentment over the harsh terms of the Treaty of Versailles fueled German nationalism in the years after World War I. This environment allowed Hitler and the Nazi Party to rise to power, pursuing an aggressive ideology of territorial expansion. Western powers responded with a policy of appeasement, which ultimately failed to stop escalating German aggression. Germany's invasion of Poland in 1939 finally triggered declarations of war from Britain and France, beginning the conflict in Europe.

Q (q24, 2 pts): Explain the significance of the Non-Aligned Movement during the Cold War.
A: The Non-Aligned Movement was significant because its member states deliberately sought to avoid formally aligning with either the U.S.-led capitalist bloc or the Soviet-led communist bloc during the Cold War. This gave newly independent and developing nations a way to pursue their own interests without being forced to pick a side. Key figures associated with this movement include Jawaharlal Nehru of India, Gamal Abdel Nasser of Egypt, and Josip Broz Tito of Yugoslavia.
