A: Killed about a third of Europe. Happened mid 1300s. Caused religious crisis. People blamed minorities, like Jews, for it. Prayer didn't stop the sickness. Made people question the Church's power.

Q (q05, 2 pts): Describe how the Aztec Empire extracted resources from the peoples it conquered.
A: The Aztec Empire mainly extracted resources through a tribute system. Conquered city-states had to regularly send goods, labor, or soldiers to the empire. This kept resources flowing in from all the different territories they controlled.

Q (q06, 3 pts): Explain how the Ottoman Empire used the devshirme system.
A: I think the devshirme system involved taking young boys, and I believe they were converted to Islam and then trained for years. From what I remember, a lot of them ended up becoming Janissary soldiers, which were like elite troops, or sometimes they became high up administrators in the government. I'm not totally sure of the exact process of how they were selected, but I think the point of doing this was so the Sultan had a military and administrative class that was loyal specifically to him, not to some noble family that might challenge him.

Q (q07, 5 pts): Explain the significance of the Atlantic Slave Trade for the development of the Americas.
A: So thinking about the Atlantic Slave Trade, it's basically this triangular trade thing connecting Europe, West Africa, and the Americas, where millions of enslaved Africans were forcibly shipped across the Atlantic, and that's just such an enormous number of people forced into this system. And once they got to the Americas a lot of them were put to work on plantations, growing cash crops like sugar, tobacco, cotton, that kind of stuff, and this labor was basically the engine that made these colonial economies run. And because of all that plantation production, European trading empires made tons and tons of money off of it, like huge wealth flowing back to places like Britain and Portugal and Spain. But at the same time, back in Africa, this whole system was draining populations from West and Central Africa over hundreds of years, which destabilized a lot of societies and kingdoms there, so it wasn't just an economic thing for Europe and the Americas, it really hurt Africa long term too. And then on the other side, in the Americas, you get this lasting African cultural influence, like in religion, music, language, food, all these things persisted and blended into American societies and you can still see it today. So overall it's this huge multi-continent system that reshaped economies and cultures on like three continents at once.

Q (q09, 4 pts): Describe the Columbian Exchange and its effects.
A: The Columbian Exchange had major effects on population growth. New World crops like maize and potatoes were brought to Afro-Eurasia and this boosted food supplies, which helped populations grow there. On the other side, European livestock and crops were brought to the Americas, and this changed American ecosystems and diets, reshaping societies in both places.

A: So the sakoku policy, I think this was mainly about the Shogunate trying to stop Christianity from spreading in Japan, and also trying to limit how much foreign countries could influence Japan politically and economically. But it wasn't a total shutdown, because I remember the Dutch were still allowed to trade, just in a really controlled way, through this port called Nagasaki, or maybe it was specifically an island there called Dejima. So it's kind of interesting that it wasn't a complete isolation, there was still this one narrow channel of contact kept open with the Dutch even while everything else was being restricted.

Q (q13, 5 pts): Describe the causes and effects of the French Revolution.
A: One cause of the French Revolution was resentment toward the estates system, since the nobility and clergy had privileges that the common people did not share. This created a lot of frustration among the lower classes. As a result of the revolution, the monarchy was overthrown, and eventually Louis XVI was executed. The instability that followed this collapse of order eventually allowed Napoleon Bonaparte to rise to power.

I'm not totally sure of all the details, but I also think the revolution inspired other enslaved and colonized people elsewhere, while at the same time it really alarmed other slaveholding powers who were scared something similar could happen to them.

Q (q18, 3 pts): Explain the significance of the Meiji Restoration in Japan.

Q (q19, 4 pts): Explain the causes of World War I.
A: One cause of World War I was the alliance system, since a conflict between just two powers could quickly pull in the rest of Europe through these interlocking agreements. Another cause was competing nationalist and imperial ambitions, which created constant tension among the great powers of Europe. There was also a prewar arms race, including naval competition between Britain and Germany, which made countries more ready and willing to go to war.
