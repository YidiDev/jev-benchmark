So overall the Indian Ocean trade network mattered for goods, for connecting distant regions, for the monsoon-based sailing technology, and for spreading Islam.

Q (q05, 2 pts): Describe how the Aztec Empire extracted resources from the peoples it conquered.
A: The Aztec Empire mainly extracted resources through a tribute system. Conquered city-states were required to regularly send goods, labor, or military service to the empire as a form of tribute. This system was efficient because it let the Aztecs fund their imperial capital, Tenochtitlan, and support the ruling elite without having to directly administer every single conquered territory themselves. So instead of installing Aztec officials everywhere, they mostly left local rulers in place as long as tribute kept flowing in.

Q (q09, 4 pts): Describe the Columbian Exchange and its effects.
A: The Columbian Exchange had several major effects across the Atlantic world. One huge effect was that Old World diseases like smallpox devastated indigenous populations in the Americas, since they had no prior immunity to these new illnesses. On the other side, New World crops such as maize and potatoes were brought to Afro-Eurasia, and these boosted food supplies there, which contributed to population growth. Additionally, European livestock and crops were introduced into the Americas, which transformed American ecosystems and diets, ultimately reshaping societies on both sides of the Atlantic.

Q (q11, 3 pts): Explain the causes of the Scientific Revolution in Europe.
A: The Scientific Revolution was caused by a shift in how people thought about gaining knowledge. There was a new emphasis on empirical observation and experimentation, meaning people wanted to test ideas directly rather than just accept old teachings. This new approach directly challenged the old reliance on classical texts, like the works of Aristotle, and also challenged religious doctrine as being the only valid source of truth about the natural world. So basically people started trusting evidence and experiments over authority and tradition.

Q (q19, 4 pts): Explain the causes of World War I.

Third, there was the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand in Sarajevo in 1914, which acted as the immediate spark for the war. Finally, a prewar arms race, including naval competition between Britain and Germany, had already heightened tensions and readiness for war before the assassination even occurred.
