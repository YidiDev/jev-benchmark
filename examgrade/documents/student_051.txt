Q (q01, 3 pts): Describe the political and administrative structure the Mongol Empire used to govern its territory.
A: The Mongol Empire was founded by Genghis Khan, who unified the steppe tribes under his rule. To govern and organize his people, he created a decimal-based military-administrative system that grouped soldiers and their families into units of 10, 100, 1,000, and 10,000. This system deliberately cut across old tribal and clan loyalties, breaking up traditional allegiances. As a result, the Mongols could quickly mobilize and command a massive, multi-ethnic population and army without depending on pre-existing tribal hierarchies that might compete with the khan's authority.

Q (q02, 4 pts): Explain the significance of the Indian Ocean trade network before 1500 CE.
A: Indian Ocean trade moved spices, textiles, porcelain, and gold. Traders connected East Africa's Swahili coast, Arabia, India, Southeast Asia, and China. Merchants and travelers also spread Islam along the East African coast and into Southeast Asia. Lots of goods and ideas moved through this network.

Q (q03, 3 pts): Describe the circumstances of the fall of the Byzantine Empire in 1453.
A: In 1453 the Ottoman Empire, led by Mehmed II, captured Constantinople, ending the Byzantine Empire. This event marked the end of the Roman imperial line that had continued through Byzantium. It also gave the Ottomans control over key Eastern Mediterranean trade routes, which pushed Europeans to search for new routes to Asia.

Q (q07, 5 pts): Explain the significance of the Atlantic Slave Trade for the development of the Americas.
A: The Atlantic Slave Trade formed a triangular trade system linking Europe, West Africa, and the Americas, forcibly transporting millions of enslaved Africans across the ocean. These enslaved people were forced into brutal labor on plantations producing cash crops like sugar, tobacco, and cotton. The wealth generated by these plantation exports enriched European trading empires and fueled the growth of colonial economies. At the same time, the slave trade depopulated and destabilized parts of West and Central Africa over several centuries, as communities were torn apart by constant raiding and warfare tied to the trade. Finally, the forced migration left a lasting legacy of African cultural, religious, and linguistic influence throughout the Americas, shaping music, food, language, and religion in the New World.

Q (q08, 3 pts): Explain the causes of the Protestant Reformation.
A: Martin Luther posted his Ninety-Five Theses in 1517 criticizing the Church. The printing press spread his ideas fast across Europe. Some German princes backed reform too, for political and economic reasons of their own.

Q (q09, 4 pts): Describe the Columbian Exchange and its effects.
A: So the Columbian Exchange, I guess it's basically this huge swap of stuff after 1492 between the Americas and Afro-Eurasia, like crops, animals, people, and unfortunately diseases too. It really was diseases like smallpox that hit indigenous populations super hard since they had no immunity built up, so their populations just collapsed in huge numbers. But going the other way, New World crops like maize and potatoes ended up boosting food supplies in Afro-Eurasia, which helped populations grow there over time. And then European livestock and crops came into the Americas and totally changed the ecosystems and diets there, so really both sides of the ocean got reshaped, societies changed on both ends because of all this exchange going back and forth.

Q (q10, 3 pts): Explain the significance of the Tokugawa Shogunate's sakoku policy.
A: Okay so sakoku was the Tokugawa Shogunate's policy of basically shutting Japan off from most of the outside world, restricting foreign trade and travel really severely. Part of the reason, I keep coming back to this, was they wanted to stop Christianity from spreading and limit how much foreign powers could influence Japan politically or economically. But it wasn't a total shutdown, because they still let the Dutch trade in a limited way through the controlled port of Nagasaki, on that island Dejima, so there was still this one narrow window to the outside world even during the isolation period.

Q (q11, 3 pts): Explain the causes of the Scientific Revolution in Europe.
A: The Scientific Revolution grew out of a new emphasis on empirical observation and experimentation as the basis for reliable knowledge. This approach challenged the older reliance on classical texts like those of Aristotle and on religious doctrine as the sole sources of truth about the natural world. Figures such as Copernicus, Galileo, and Newton drove this shift, with discoveries like heliocentrism directly contradicting long-held Church-endorsed views of the universe.

Q (q12, 4 pts): Explain how Enlightenment political philosophy influenced later revolutions.
A: So Enlightenment thinkers came up with these big ideas like natural rights, the social contract, and separation of powers, and people like Locke, Rousseau, and Montesquieu were central to spreading them around. These ideas then fed directly into the American Revolution, since colonists used natural rights and social contract arguments to justify breaking away and setting up self-government. And then again with the French Revolution, similar ideas about liberty, equality, and getting rid of absolute monarchy show up, so really the same Enlightenment concepts kept circling back and showing up in both revolutions, just applied to different situations on each side of the Atlantic.

A: The Great Depression began with the 1929 U.S. stock market crash. Because global trade and lending were so interconnected, the crisis spread rapidly to other economies around the world. This led to mass unemployment and severe hardship on a global scale, affecting millions of ordinary people.

Q (q24, 2 pts): Explain the significance of the Non-Aligned Movement during the Cold War.

Figures like Nehru from India, Nasser from Egypt, and Tito from Yugoslavia were kind of the faces of this movement, pushing this third path. So really it kept coming back to this idea of not choosing a side, staying neutral-ish between the two superpowers.
