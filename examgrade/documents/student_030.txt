Q (q01, 3 pts): Describe the political and administrative structure the Mongol Empire used to govern its territory.
A: The Mongol Empire was governed mainly through a religious council system where local priests reported directly to the Great Khan every year. Each region had to send delegates to Karakorum where they would vote on trade taxes. The empire also relied heavily on a written constitution that limited the khan's power, similar to how feudal Europe worked. Because of this, the empire was very decentralized and each city basically ran itself like a small democracy under Mongol supervision.

Q (q04, 3 pts): Explain the impact of the Black Death on European society and economy.
A: The Black Death had a major impact on the labor supply in Europe. So many workers died that there were not enough peasants left to work the land for the lords. This meant that the surviving laborers could demand higher wages since their work was now more valuable and scarce. As a result, the manorial system and serfdom, which depended on lords having a large captive labor force, started to break down. This helped speed up the decline of feudalism in Europe as peasants gained more bargaining power and mobility.

Q (q06, 3 pts): Explain how the Ottoman Empire used the devshirme system.
A: The devshirme system was a policy where the Ottoman government forced all citizens to convert to Islam within one generation or pay extra taxes forever. It mainly affected merchants and traders in Istanbul, requiring them to give up a portion of their goods every year to the sultan's treasury. This system was used to fund the building of mosques and palaces, and it made the Ottoman economy grow because more people were paying into the treasury. It was similar to a tithe system found in medieval Europe.
