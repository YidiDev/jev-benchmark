Q (q01, 3 pts): Describe the political and administrative structure the Mongol Empire used to govern its territory.
A: The Mongol Empire was founded by Genghis Khan, who unified the Mongol tribes and built the empire from there. One key part of how it worked was that it let the Mongols quickly mobilize and command a huge population made up of many different ethnic groups, without having to depend on the old tribal or clan hierarchies that existed before. This meant the empire could organize soldiers and subjects from many backgrounds efficiently across a massive territory.

Q (q02, 4 pts): Explain the significance of the Indian Ocean trade network before 1500 CE.
A: The Indian Ocean trade network carried valuable goods like spices, textiles, porcelain, and gold across long distances. Sailors were able to plan reliable round trip voyages because the monsoon winds reversed direction predictably with the seasons, so ships could ride one way and come back later on the opposite wind. This network also spread religion, since Islam spread along the East African coast and into Southeast Asia through merchants and travelers who settled and did business there. Overall this shows how trade wasn't just economic but also carried culture and religion with it.

A: I think the fall of Constantinople in 1453 happened when the Ottoman Empire, led by Mehmed II, finally captured the city after a long siege. I'm not totally sure of all the details, but I believe this event mattered a lot because it ended the line of the Roman Empire that had continued through Byzantium, and it gave the Ottomans control over important trade routes in the Eastern Mediterranean. Because of that, I think Europeans started looking for new ocean routes to Asia so they wouldn't have to rely on Ottoman-controlled paths anymore.

Q (q04, 3 pts): Explain the impact of the Black Death on European society and economy.
A: The Black Death hit Europe hard. It killed about a third of the population in the mid-1300s. Fewer workers meant labor got scarce. Wages rose. Serfdom and the manorial system weakened because lords needed to compete for workers. This helped feudalism decline. The plague also caused religious panic. People blamed minority groups and turned to scapegoating. Prayer didn't stop the disease, so many started doubting the Church's authority. Big social, economic, and religious shifts all at once.

Q (q06, 3 pts): Explain how the Ottoman Empire used the devshirme system.
A: The devshirme system took boys, mostly from Christian families in the Balkans, and brought them into Ottoman service. They were converted to Islam and given training, and many of them ended up becoming elite Janissary soldiers or rose into high administrative positions. This mattered because it created a military and bureaucratic class that owed loyalty directly to the Sultan instead of to hereditary noble families, which helped centralize Ottoman power.

Q (q07, 5 pts): Explain the significance of the Atlantic Slave Trade for the development of the Americas.
A: So the Atlantic Slave Trade, when I think about it, was basically this huge triangular system linking Europe, West Africa, and the Americas together, and through it millions of enslaved Africans were forcibly shipped across the Atlantic. It's kind of hard to wrap my head around the scale but yeah, millions of people forced across the ocean. This trade generated enormous wealth for European powers and their colonies, like the plantation exports fueled whole economies back in Europe, so it was massively profitable for the empires running it. At the same time, and I keep coming back to this, it depopulated and destabilized parts of West and Central Africa over a long stretch of time, like centuries, pulling away generations of people and disrupting societies there. And then on the other side of the ocean, in the Americas, you get this lasting African cultural, religious, and linguistic influence that shaped music, food, religion, language, all sorts of things across American societies. So really it was significant in like four different directions at once, tying regions together economically while also causing devastation in Africa and reshaping culture in the Americas.

Q (q09, 4 pts): Describe the Columbian Exchange and its effects.
A: I think the Columbian Exchange refers to the transfer of crops, livestock, people, and diseases between the Americas and Afro-Eurasia after Columbus arrived in 1492. I'm not totally sure I have every detail right, but I believe one of the biggest effects was that Old World diseases like smallpox spread among indigenous peoples in the Americas who had no immunity, which caused massive population loss. On the other side, I think New World crops like maize and potatoes traveled to Afro-Eurasia and helped boost food supplies there, which led to population growth in places like Europe and Asia. Meanwhile, European livestock and crops came into the Americas and changed the ecosystems and diets there, so overall both sides of the Atlantic were reshaped by this exchange in pretty dramatic ways.

Q (q11, 3 pts): Explain the causes of the Scientific Revolution in Europe.
A: The Scientific Revolution grew out of a new emphasis on empirical observation and experimentation as the proper basis for knowledge. This approach directly challenged the older reliance on classical texts like those of Aristotle, as well as on religious doctrine, as the only accepted sources of truth. Key figures such as Copernicus, Galileo, and Newton drove this shift, with discoveries like heliocentrism showing that careful observation could overturn long-held beliefs about the universe.

Both revolutions leaned on these core ideas.

Q (q13, 5 pts): Describe the causes and effects of the French Revolution.
A: The French Revolution had several causes. The French government faced chronic debt, made worse by costly wars like support for the American Revolution, which created a fiscal crisis. There was also deep resentment of the rigid estates system, since the nobility and clergy enjoyed privileges like tax exemptions while the common people bore the burden. Enlightenment ideas about liberty, equality, and popular sovereignty gave people a language and framework to challenge the old order. As a result of the revolution, the monarchy was overthrown and King Louis XVI was eventually executed. The instability that followed, with shifting governments and radical phases, eventually opened the door for Napoleon Bonaparte to rise to power.

Q (q14, 4 pts): Explain the significance of the Haitian Revolution.
A: So the Haitian Revolution, thinking about it, was led by Toussaint Louverture who guided the uprising of the enslaved population against colonial rule, and that's kind of the starting point of the whole thing. What makes it stand out, and I keep circling back to this, is that it was the only successful large-scale slave revolt that actually led to founding an independent nation, which became Haiti. That's a huge deal when you think about how many other revolts happened but didn't result in an actual new independent country. Beyond just Haiti itself, the revolution inspired enslaved and colonized people in other places who saw that it was possible to overthrow their oppressors, and at the same time it really alarmed slaveholding powers elsewhere, like in the US and other Caribbean colonies, because they were scared of similar revolts happening in their own territories.

Q (q20, 4 pts): Explain the significance of the Bolshevik Revolution.
A: The Bolshevik Revolution occurred in 1917 when Vladimir Lenin and the Bolshevik Party seized power in Russia. They overthrew the provisional government, which had itself only recently replaced the Tsar earlier that same year. Once in power, the Bolsheviks pulled Russia out of World War I and began constructing a Communist state. This was significant globally because it created the world's first major Communist state, which both inspired revolutionary movements elsewhere and deeply alarmed capitalist governments around the world.
