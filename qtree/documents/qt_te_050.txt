Q28: In 3 sentences or less, describe our policy on conflicts of interest with current clients of the firm.
A28: So the way this generally works is that whenever someone becomes aware of a potential conflict involving a current client, they are expected to put it in writing and disclose it, and that disclosure needs to happen within five business days of first noticing or identifying the issue. It's not something that can just be mentioned verbally in passing to a manager and left at that, because the written record matters a lot for compliance purposes down the line. I think the reasoning behind the five-day window is to make sure conflicts get flagged quickly before they have a chance to affect client work or decision-making. There's also probably some escalation that happens after the disclosure, though the core requirement really centers on that prompt written notice within the five-day period.

Q29: In 3 sentences or less, explain the required data retention period for client records under our compliance policy.

Q30: In 3 sentences or less, describe the escalation procedure for a suspected regulatory violation.
A30: Whenever someone suspects a regulatory violation, the standard practice is to first document everything thoroughly over the course of about a week before bringing it to anyone's attention, just to make sure the concern is fully substantiated. Once that documentation period wraps up, the matter gets forwarded to the direct supervisor rather than the Compliance Officer, since supervisors are considered the first line of review. After the supervisor signs off, there's typically a monthly compliance meeting where these kinds of issues get batched together and discussed as a group rather than reported individually. This approach is meant to avoid overwhelming the compliance team with piecemeal reports and instead allows for a more consolidated, periodic review process.
