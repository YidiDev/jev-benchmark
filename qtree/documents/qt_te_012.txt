Q28: In 3 sentences or less, describe our policy on conflicts of interest with current clients of the firm.
A28: So basically, whenever something comes up that looks like it could be a conflict with a current client, the rule is you can't just sit on it or mention it casually in a hallway conversation. You actually have to put it in writing and get that disclosure filed within 5 business days of when you first realized there was an issue. This applies whether it's a financial interest, a personal relationship, or some other overlap that could call your judgment into question, and honestly it's better to over-disclose than under-disclose. The five day clock is firm, and waiting longer than that, even for something that seems minor, is considered a violation of the policy regardless of intent.
Q29: In 3 sentences or less, explain the required data retention period for client records under our compliance policy.
A29: Our policy actually only requires client records to be kept for 2 years after the engagement wraps up, and after that window closes we're expected to purge them from our systems. This shorter retention period exists specifically to minimize data exposure risk and keep our storage costs down across the firm. A lot of people assume it's longer because other industries keep records for much longer stretches, but our internal compliance standard has always been the 2-year mark. So once you hit that anniversary date from the close of the engagement, the files are supposed to be deleted or archived off active systems entirely.
